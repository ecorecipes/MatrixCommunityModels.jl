MatrixCommunityModels.cypripedium_model — Method
cypripedium_model(; equivalence = true, integeronly = false, dir = joinpath(DATA_DIR, "cypripedium"))

The two-orchid community of the adapt3 community-projection vignette (Shefferson 2026): Cypripedium candidum (5 annual matrices) and C. parviflorum (17), nine stages each, cycled through in order. The species are linked by one pool, density, the number of individuals of both species, not counting dormant seed when equivalence is true. Production of dormant seed (SD) and of protocorms (P1) by flowering plants is multiplied by the Ricker terms 0.5 exp(-density) and 1.2 exp(-2 density), using RickerDensity from MatrixProjectionModels.jl. With integeronly the stage vectors are floored each step, as in adapt3.

The annual matrices were estimated in R with lefko3 and are shipped as data. Returns (community, n0, envs); the environment of step t is the integer t - 1, which selects each species' matrix for that year.

MatrixCommunityModels.riparian_model — Method
riparian_model(; corrected = false, guilds = RIPARIAN_GUILDS, path = ...)

The five-guild riparian vegetation community of Lytle et al. (2017). Guilds are linked by one pool, space: the area occupied after the year's flood and drought mortality, in cottonwood-seedling units. Recruitment of every guild is proportional to the free space K - space.

With corrected = false the model reproduces the published R script exactly, including two behaviours that look unintended: the meadow guild receives the willow guild's recruitment (expressed here as a second pool, willow_adults), and sagebrush and meadow lose the stage-5 individuals that do not mature. corrected = true gives meadow its own recruitment, gated on its own adults, and keeps those stage-5 individuals. Both variants keep the script's floor of 10 individuals per sagebrush stage.

guilds supplies the parameters; pass a copy changed with with_rate to perturb a vital rate.

Returns (community, n0, envs, K); envs holds one environment per year of the 1916-1998 Yampa River record at Maybell.

MatrixCommunityModels.verde_fish_model — Method
verde_fish_model(; K = 47660.0, dir = joinpath(DATA_DIR, "verde_fish"))

The seven-species Verde River fish community of Rogosch et al. (2019): three-stage biomass models linked by one pool, total community biomass, which limits every species' fecundity through max(0, 1 - biomass / K).

Returns (community, n0, envs, years, density): the composed community, the initial biomass (the stage-3 values of the vital-rate table), one environment per year of the 1964-2017 flow record (year-type flags), the years, and the individuals-per-gram conversion for each species' stages.

MatrixCommunityModels.bumblebee — Method
bumblebee(; virus = false, parameters = BUMBLEBEE_PARAMETERS, forage = nothing,
            flowers = nothing, name = :bumblebee)

A population of bumblebee (Bombus terrestris) colonies on a daily time step; numbers can be read as per square kilometre.

Queens hibernate, emerge in spring, search for a nest site and found a nest alone; after founding_days the nest has its first workers. A colony first rears workers (growing), then males and new queens (reproducing), then stops (ending). The number of nests is limited by nest_sites. Worker, male and gyne brood take 22, 26 and 30 days. New queens mate and stay dormant, joining the hibernating queens when the season ends; everything else dies then. Colonies are pooled: workers per colony is the total number of workers over the total number of colonies.

Bumblebees keep almost no stores, so each day's intake is compared with each day's need: larvae are lost when pollen or nectar is short (gyne larvae more readily), and foragers die faster when nectar is hard to find.

With virus = true there are infected workers, infected at flowers with the daily probability given by flowers (from flower_transmission); they die infected_hazard times as fast. There is no transmission within the nest, and no mites.

forage is the result of floral_competition, if the population shares flowers.

MatrixCommunityModels.flight_index — Method
flight_index(day; threshold = 10, full = 18)

Fraction of a day of the year on which bees can fly, between 0 and 1: flight rises linearly as the daily maximum temperature goes from threshold to full °C. The temperature is an illustrative temperate climate (14 ± 11 °C, warmest in late July), not fitted to a site. Bumblebees fly in cooler weather (MacQueen et al. 2025); use threshold = 6, full = 12 for them.

MatrixCommunityModels.floral_competition — Function
floral_competition(mode = :scramble; nectar, pollen, hives = 1, removal = 1,
                   overlap = 0.5, honeybee_share = 0.5, refuge = 0.2, carryover = 0.5)

Link honey bees and bumblebees through floral nectar and pollen. nectar and pollen are supply functions of the day of the year (grams of sugar and of pollen per day, see floral_supply). hives is the number of honey bee colonies drawing on the supply (per square kilometre, if bumblebees are per square kilometre), each identical to the one modelled; removal converts a gram of honey gained into grams of floral sugar removed (a colony collects about 120 kg of nectar a year, some 48 kg of sugar, which is the sugar in about 60 kg of honey: roughly 0.8 to 1).

The default supplies peak at 3 kg of sugar and 3 kg of pollen per square kilometre per day. That is of the order measured for intensively farmed land; flower-rich habitat offers 3 to 60 times as much (Hicks et al. 2016; Baude et al. 2016), with pollen and sugar in roughly equal mass.

Each species reports its demand to the pools hb_nectar, hb_pollen, bb_nectar and bb_pollen, and receives the fraction of its demand that is met. mode decides how:

  • :scramble: everyone gets the same fraction, min(1, supply / total demand).
  • :standing_crop: the supply is a stock (a flowers species, pools nectar_stock and pollen_stock) that is secreted daily and of which a fraction carryover of what is not removed persists, so heavy use one day lowers what is there the next.
  • :partitioned: a fraction overlap of the supply is open to both species; the rest is exclusive, a share honeybee_share of it to honey bees and the remainder to bumblebees.
  • :preemptive: honey bees take what they want first, except from a fraction refuge of the supply that only bumblebees can use (deep flowers); bumblebees get what is left.

Pass the result as forage to honeybee and bumblebee, and add its species (the flowers, if any) to the community.

MatrixCommunityModels.flower_transmission — Method
flower_transmission(; flowers = floral_supply(5.0e6), hives = 1, honeybee_visits = 500,
                    deposit = 0.02, bumblebee_deposit = 0.0, acquisition = 1.0)

Link honey bees and bumblebees through virus left on shared flowers. Infected foragers report their visits to the pool infectious_visits (bumblebee visits weighted by bumblebee_deposit, zero by default: bumblebees pick the virus up but do not pass it on). The fraction of flowers contaminated on a day is 1 - exp(-deposit × infectious visits / flowers(day)), so more flowers dilute the virus. A bumblebee worker is infected with daily probability 1 - exp(-acquisition × contamination); a honey bee colony's exposure is the contamination itself, which matters only if its oral_susceptibility is above zero.

None of these rates has been measured. deposit is set so that an infected forager leaves virus on about ten flowers a day (the model fitted by Burnham et al. 2021 has four a day, lasting ten days), and acquisition so that roughly a tenth of bumblebee workers carry the virus beside an untreated colony, as in field surveys (Fürst et al. 2014; Alger et al. 2019; Manley et al. 2019). Only their product matters to the bumblebees.

Pass the result as flowers to honeybee and bumblebee.

MatrixCommunityModels.honeybee — Method
honeybee(; virus = false, drones = true, parameters = HONEYBEE_PARAMETERS, forage = nothing,
           flowers = nothing, oral_susceptibility = 0.0, name = :honeybee)

The bees of a honey bee (Apis mellifera) colony on a daily time step.

The workers' life cycle is egg → larva → pupa → hive → forager, with eggs, larvae and pupae lasting 3, 5 and 12 days. The queen's laying follows hopomo_season and is capped by the brood the adults can tend; larvae are lost when nurses or pollen are short; hive bees become foragers at the rate of Khoury et al. (2013) on flying days while there is brood, so bees emerging after brood rearing stops become long-lived winter bees.

With drones = true a second life cycle, drone_egg → drone_larva → drone_pupa → drone (3, 7 and 14 days), is merged in: from late April to late August the queen lays drone_eggs drone eggs for each worker egg. Drones eat and need nursing but do no work. They matter because mites prefer, and breed better in, drone cells.

The colony's honey and pollen (grams) are two further entries of its state, added as a module: each day the stores lose what the bees eat and gain what the foragers bring in, and the share of foragers collecting pollen rises when the pollen store is low.

With virus = true the colony has three strata: healthy, carrier and infected. A larva becomes infected when an infectious mite enters its cell at capping (pool viral_phoretic, from varroa); a fraction of infected pupae die, and the adults they become die faster. A healthy in-hive bee becomes a carrier when an infectious mite takes it as a host (pools viral_phoretic and viral_emerging); as in BEEHAVE, a bee infected as an adult lives as long as a healthy one but passes the virus to mites. oral_susceptibility above zero lets adults become carriers from virus on flowers (see flower_transmission, passed as flowers).

forage is the result of floral_competition, if the colony shares flowers.

MatrixCommunityModels.varroa — Method
varroa(; virus = false, drones = true, parameters = VARROA_PARAMETERS, treatment = day -> 0.0, name = :varroa)

The mite Varroa destructor in a honey bee colony. A mite is phoretic (on an adult bee) or in a brood cell, a worker cell for 12 days or a drone_cell for 15, until the bee emerges; it then leaves with its daughters. Each day a fraction of the phoretic mites enters the cells about to be capped; the fractions depend on the pools capping, drone_capping and adult_bees reported by honeybee, and a cell takes at most four mites. Mites enter drone cells almost twelve times as readily as worker cells and leave nearly three times as many daughters there. Phoretic mites die faster while the colony has brood (pool brood). drones must be the same as in honeybee.

With virus = true mites are stratified into clean and viral. A clean mite becomes viral by entering a cell whose pupa is infected, or by taking a bee that carries the virus as its host (pools host_turnover, hive_bees, infected_hive_bees). Taking a host follows BEEHAVE: a clean mite that takes a new host, because it has just left a cell or because its host died or became a forager, becomes viral with a probability equal to the fraction of in-hive bees carrying the virus. A viral mite stays viral. Its daughters are viral if the pupa in their cell was infected and lived; if it died there are no daughters, and if it escaped infection they are clean.

treatment(day_of_year) is an extra daily mortality of phoretic mites. infection_per_cell, pupal_death and brood_survival (the chance that worker and drone brood survive the days a mite spends in the cell) repeat values of the honey bee model and default to them.

MatrixCommunityModels.flowerhead_weevil — Method
flowerhead_weevil(; parameters = WEEVIL_PARAMETERS, name = :weevil)

The flower-head weevil Rhinocyllus conicus, released against musk thistle and now also on native thistles. It has one generation a year: adults that have overwintered lay eggs on flower heads in spring, larvae feed in the head, and new adults overwinter. The state is the number of adults in spring.

The weevil lays its eggs into the pool eggs; thistles share them out among the heads they offer. Larvae compete within heads: the more eggs there are for the weevils that the heads can rear (the pool rearing, supplied by the thistles), the smaller the share that develops. No thistle is named: any species that offers heads and rearing is a host.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.musk_thistle — Method
musk_thistle(; parameters = MUSK_THISTLE_PARAMETERS, weevil_survival = WEEVIL_PARAMETERS.survival,
               name = :musk_thistle)

Musk thistle (Carduus nutans), an invasive monocarpic thistle, as the matrix model of Jongejans et al. (2008) written from its vital rates: a seed bank and small, medium and large rosettes, counted at the end of autumn. Transitions are dormancy, emerging (from the seed bank), growing (rosettes that survive without bolting), and seed_rain and establishing (the seeds of plants that bolt, entering the seed bank or becoming rosettes).

Two responses are added to the published model:

  • New rosettes need open ground: the seedlings of a year compete for the sites that hold no rosette, and of seedlings contending for free sites the share free / (free + seedlings) establishes (a response on emerging and establishing). This bounds a population that would otherwise grow 1.75-fold a year, and sites sets how abundant the weed is.
  • The flower-head weevil destroys seed. The published model has a multiplier for the share of seeds escaping floral herbivory; here it follows from the pools. Bolting plants offer their early heads to the pool heads (each worth attraction Platte thistle heads) and the weevils these can rear to the pool rearing; each weevil larva that develops destroys seeds_per_larva seeds. weevil_survival is the chance that an egg yields a developing larva, repeated from flowerhead_weevil.

MatrixCommunityModels.platte_thistle — Method
platte_thistle(; parameters = PLATTE_THISTLE_PARAMETERS, cells = 100, native_insects = true,
                 seed_limited = true, aggregated = true, eggs = nothing, name = :platte_thistle)

Platte thistle (Cirsium canescens), a monocarpic thistle of Nebraska sand prairie, as the integral projection model of Rose, Louda & Rees (2005). Plants are structured by size on cells mesh cells (see platte_thistle_stage); numbers are plants on 720 m². Transitions are growing (survive, do not flower, change size) and seeding (survive, flower, set seed, and the seedlings that result).

  • native_insects: native flower-head insects destroy a fixed share of the seeds.
  • seed_limited: recruits are a power of the seeds produced, as fitted, so a seed is less likely to establish in a large crop; the chance is capped at the value measured for seeds sown at low density. With false every seed has the same chance, which gives a growth rate.
  • The flower-head weevil reduces seed set according to the eggs on the plant. With eggs = nothing these follow from the pools: the plant offers its flower heads to the pool heads and receives a share of the pool eggs in proportion, so that larger plants carry more eggs. It also adds the weevils its heads can rear to the pool rearing. With eggs a function of size, that many eggs are imposed instead.
  • aggregated: eggs are spread unevenly among plants of the same size (a negative binomial distribution), as observed; with false every plant of a size carries the mean.

Alone, or with nothing laying eggs, the thistle has no weevil damage. Compose it with flowerhead_weevil and musk_thistle.

Forest trees

MatrixCommunityModels.FOREST_CLIMATE — Constant
FOREST_CLIMATE

Default climate of forest_tree: the yearly sum of degree days above 5.5 °C (sgdd) and the water availability index (wai: precipitation minus potential evapotranspiration, divided by potential evapotranspiration) at the middle of the climatic range of Norway spruce. The values are those of the R package matreex (its table climate_species, Picea abies, N == 2), the climate of its introductory vignette.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.FOREST_PARAMETERS — Constant
FOREST_PARAMETERS

Fitted vital rates of three European trees, for forest_tree: Norway spruce (spruce, Picea abies), silver fir (fir, Abies alba) and European beech (beech, Fagus sylvatica). The numbers are those shipped with the R package matreex 0.4.0 (objects fit_Picea_abies, fit_Abies_alba and fit_Fagus_sylvatica; MIT licence), copied with all their digits.

For each species: largest, the largest trunk diameter in the data (mm); delay, the years a recruit takes to reach the smallest diameter recorded; and three regressions, each a list of term => coefficient:

  • growth: the mean of the logarithm of a tree's diameter growth in a year (mm), which is normally distributed with standard deviation sigma;
  • death: the complementary log-log of the chance that a tree dies in a year;
  • recruitment: the logarithm of the number of recruits in a year on a plot of 300 m².

A term is a product of variables, written as in matreex: size (diameter, mm), logsize, BATOTcomp (basal area of the stand, m² per hectare), BATOTSP and logBATOTSP (basal area of the species), BATOTNonSP (basal area of the other species), and the climate variables sgdd, sgdd2 (its square), sgddb (its inverse), wai, wai2 (its square) and waib (the inverse of 1 + wai); see FOREST_CLIMATE.

Growth and survival were fitted to European forest inventories by Kunstler et al. (2021) and are the means of their 100 fits to resampled data. The recruitment coefficients are those of matreex 0.4.0, which differ from the fits published with Guyennon et al. (2023).

MatrixCommunityModels.forest_tree — Method
forest_tree(kind; parameters = FOREST_PARAMETERS, climate = FOREST_CLIMATE, cells = 700,
              delay = parameters[kind].delay, harvest = 0.006, conspecific = nothing, name = kind,
              tabulate = nothing)

A tree species (:spruce, :fir or :beech) as the integral projection model of the R package matreex (Kunstler et al. 2021; Guyennon et al. 2023). Trees are structured by trunk diameter on cells mesh cells (see forest_stage); numbers are trees per hectare and the time step is a year. The species contributes the cross-sectional area of its trunks to the pool basal_area (m² per hectare) and reads the pool's total: a species is affected by the others only through it.

Stages are seedling, sapling and tree. Transitions are

  • growing: a tree survives, is not felled, and grows. Growth is log-normal; its mean falls and the chance of death changes as the basal area of the stand rises, so the kernel changes shape with the pool and is rebuilt each year (responsive).
  • recruiting: the species gains recruits according to its own basal area and that of the other species. They are shared among its trees in proportion to their basal area.
  • establishing and ingrowth: a recruit joins the smallest trees delay years later, without loss on the way. As in matreex, half of a year's recruits are a year ahead: they start as saplings, the other half as seedlings.

Keywords:

  • climate: a NamedTuple with sgdd and wai; see FOREST_CLIMATE.
  • delay: years from recruitment to the smallest diameter, at least 2.
  • harvest: the share of the trees felled each year, after growth and before recruitment. The default is that of matreex.
  • conspecific: the basal area that recruitment takes the species to hold, as part of the pool's total. With nothing it is the basal area of the species' own trees, and a species without trees has no recruits. Give a number to hold it fixed whatever the numbers of trees: the species is then linear, and has a growth rate (invasion_growth_rate). That describes a rare invader, whose recruits for each unit of basal area depend on how rare it is (Guyennon et al. 2023 used 0.1); it also finds the basal area at which a species alone replaces itself, where the growth rate with conspecific equal to the pool's total is one.
  • tabulate: nothing (the default) rebuilds the growth kernel every year at the basal area of the stand. Give a grid of basal areas, such as 0:1:80, to build it once at each of them and interpolate linearly in between, as matreex does (tabulated); a basal area outside the grid gets the kernel built for it. On 700 cells a year of advance is then about five times faster. A kernel is kept for each grid value the stand reaches, 0.2 to 0.9 MB each (at most about 40 MB per species for 0:1:80). With a grid of whole basal areas the basal area of the stand stays within 1e-4 of itself of that of the exact run.

Growth is integrated exactly over the cell a tree arrives in, and by a three-point rule over its place in the cell it leaves, which is the scheme of matreex. Trees that outgrow the mesh are lost.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.forest_vital_rates — Method
forest_vital_rates(kind; parameters = FOREST_PARAMETERS, climate = FOREST_CLIMATE)

The fitted vital rates of the tree kind (:spruce, :fir or :beech) at one climate, as functions of trunk diameter d (mm) and basal area (m² per hectare):

  • log_increment(d, basal_area): the mean of the logarithm of the diameter growth of a tree in a year (mm). The logarithm is normally distributed with standard deviation sigma, so the median growth is exp(log_increment(d, basal_area)).
  • death(d, basal_area): the chance that a tree dies in a year.
  • recruits(own, others): recruits per hectare and year, for a species that holds the basal area own among other species that hold others.

climate is a NamedTuple with sgdd and wai; see FOREST_CLIMATE.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.SEA_FAN_PHASES — Constant
SEA_FAN_PHASES

The three phases of the aspergillosis epizootic in sea_fan, after Bruno et al. (2011): pre_epizootic (before 1994), peak (the outbreak, fitted to surveys of 1997 to 1999 in the Florida Keys) and endemic (after the peak, from colonies followed in the Florida Keys and the Yucatán from 2002 to 2005). A phase is the environment of a time step, so an epizootic is a sequence of phases:

reef = sea_fan()
project(reef, n0, [fill(SEA_FAN_PHASES.peak, 6); fill(SEA_FAN_PHASES.endemic, 8)])

Each phase holds

  • small_fan_risk, large_fan_risk, risk_per_cm: the chance that a healthy fan is infected within the year. It is small_fan_risk up to the size large_fan of SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS, and above it large_fan_risk plus risk_per_cm for each cm of height above risk_height, which is 20 cm;
  • recovery: the chance that an infected fan that survives the year is healthy at its end;
  • disease_mortality: mortality of infected fans of any size, added to the mortality that depends on size;
  • recruitment: a multiplier of the recruits produced, which were far fewer before 2003.

A phase is a NamedTuple, so a variant is made with merge, for example merge(SEA_FAN_PHASES.endemic, (recovery = 0.2,)).

MatrixCommunityModels.sea_fan — Method
sea_fan(; parameters = SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS, cells = 100, resistant = false,
          outside = 1.0, transmission = 0.0, name = :sea_fan)

The sea fan Gorgonia ventalina with aspergillosis, as the integral projection model of Bruno et al. (2011): colonies are classified by size (see sea_fan_stage) and by infection status. Numbers are colonies per m² of suitable habitat and the time step is a year.

The life cycle is that of a healthy fan, which survives and grows (growing) and, if 20 cm or taller, produces recruits (recruiting), with the kernels of an infected fan that survives beside it: one that recovers (recovering) and one that stays infected (lingering). stratify makes the strata healthy and infected: a healthy fan that grows stays healthy or is infected, and recovering leads back, so the stages are fan1_healthy, ..., fan1_infected, ... and the transitions growing_healthy, growing_healthy_to_infected, recovering_infected_to_healthy, lingering_infected and recruiting_healthy. The chance of each fate at each size is then attached to the transitions that leave one stratum, with by_trait(...; stratum = ...).

The environment of a time step is the phase of the epizootic, one of SEA_FAN_PHASES or a variant of one; with nothing the phase is endemic.

  • resistant = true adds a third stratum, resistant, of fans that cannot be infected and whose recruits are resistant too: they have the life cycle of a healthy fan (growing_resistant, recruiting_resistant) and are never infected. resistant = :only gives resistant fans alone, to be composed with the others as a species of their own.
  • The species adds its healthy tissue, in cm², to the pool tissue, and that of its infected fans to the pool infectious.
  • outside and transmission say where the infection comes from. The chance of infection given by the phase is multiplied by outside + transmission * infectious / tissue, read from the pools. With the defaults all infection comes from outside the reef and the model is the published one, which is linear. transmission > 0 makes a share of it depend on the share of tissue that is infected; the paper has no such term and gives no value.

Sagebrush steppe

MatrixCommunityModels.SAGEBRUSH_PARAMETERS — Constant
SAGEBRUSH_PARAMETERS

Fitted vital rates of four perennial plants of the sagebrush steppe in Idaho, for sagebrush_plant: the shrub Artemisia tripartita (artr) and the bunchgrasses Hesperostipa comata (heco), Poa secunda (pose) and Pseudoroegneria spicata (pssp). They are the parameters of the Idaho example of the R package IPMdoit (Tredennick & Adler; MIT licence), read from data/sagebrush.

Size u is the logarithm of a genet's basal area in cm². For each species:

  • growth: the log area next year is normal with mean intercept + slope u + Σⱼ crowding[j] wⱼ and variance sigma_a exp(sigma_b mean);
  • survival: the logit of survival is intercept + slope u + Σⱼ crowding[j] wⱼ;
  • recruitment: recruits per m² are C exp(intercept + Σⱼ crowding[j] √Cⱼ), where Cⱼ is the percent cover of species j and C that of the species itself; recruits have a log area that is normal with size_mean and size_variance, truncated below at -1.61;
  • alpha: the spatial scale of crowding, by neighbour species, in cm⁻² (wⱼ sums the areas of neighbours of species j, each weighted by exp(-alpha r²) at distance r);
  • intercept_year, slope_year: the effects of the 22 years of the data, used when the environment of a step is a year (see sagebrush_plant).

crowding[j] is the effect of species j, in the order artr, heco, pose, pssp. Also largest, the largest genet in the data (cm²), and cells, the number of mesh cells.

MatrixCommunityModels.sagebrush_crowding — Method
sagebrush_crowding(kind, plants, cover; parameters = SAGEBRUSH_PARAMETERS, cells = ...)

The crowding experienced by genets of species kind of each size: a NamedTuple with growth and survival, each a matrix with one row for each cell of sagebrush_stage and one column for each neighbour species (artr, heco, pose, pssp). plants are the numbers of the species' own genets per m² in the cells of its stage, and cover the cover of every species (a NamedTuple with fields artr_cover, ..., as the pools of sagebrush_plant).

Crowding by another species j is π Cⱼ / alpha, with Cⱼ its cover as a share of the ground: its genets are spread at random and may overlap the focal genet. Crowding by the species' own genets, which do not overlap the focal genet, depends on their sizes and on the focal genet's radius. Both follow IPMdoit (crowd_overlap).

MatrixCommunityModels.sagebrush_plant — Method
sagebrush_plant(kind; parameters = SAGEBRUSH_PARAMETERS, cells = parameters[kind].cells,
                  niche_differences = true, name = kind)

A perennial plant of the sagebrush steppe (:artr, :heco, :pose or :pssp; see SAGEBRUSH_PARAMETERS) as the integral projection model of Adler, Ellner & Levine (2010), with the parameters and numerical scheme of the R package IPMdoit. Genets are structured by the logarithm of their area (see sagebrush_stage); numbers are genets per m² and the time step is a year.

The species adds its basal area, as a share of the ground, to the pool <kind>_cover (artr_cover, ...) and reads the cover of the other three. It has two transitions:

  • growing: a genet survives and grows. Both depend on the crowding of the genet by neighbours of each species (sagebrush_crowding), which depends on the genet's size, so the kernel is rebuilt each year (responsive).
  • recruiting: the species' recruits, C exp(intercept + Σⱼ crowding[j] √Cⱼ) per m² for percent covers C, are shared among its genets in proportion to their area. They are marked as births.

The environment of a time step is nothing, for the mean year (the random year effects set to zero, and the recruitment intercept at its posterior mean), or a year 1 to 22 of the data, for that year's effects on growth, survival and recruitment.

With niche_differences = false every species is affected by the cover of each species as by its own, which is an assumption of this package (see the vignette). In recruitment, which reads the covers, the coefficient of each neighbour species becomes the species' coefficient on itself. In growth and survival, where crowding by species j is measured on the spatial scale alpha[j], it becomes the species' own coefficient times alpha[j] / alpha[i], so that a unit of cover of any species, placed at random, crowds a plant as a unit of its own species' cover would at its own scale. The rule that conspecific genets do not overlap is kept. niche_differences can also list the vital rates that keep their niche differences, for example (:growth, :survival) to remove them from recruitment alone.
