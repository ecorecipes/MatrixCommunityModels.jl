API reference

MatrixCommunityModels.MatrixCommunityModels — Module
MatrixCommunityModels

Matrix community models (Lytle & Tonkin 2023) built from the ecorecipes categorical stack.

A species is a projection net from CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl: named stages, named transitions, and responses saying how a transition's values depend on shared pools and the environment. A community is a composite of species along an undirected wiring diagram whose junctions are the pools (space, biomass, parasites per host, ...). The composite is a PooledProjectionSystem, which is lowered to a CoupledMPMProblem and solved by MatrixProjectionModels.jl.

This package adds the composition along wiring diagrams, the analysis of the composed community, and a set of published models.

MatrixCommunityModels.abundance_sensitivity — Method
abundance_sensitivity(build, values, envs; burnin = 0, replicates = 1)

The simulation-based sensitivity of Lytle & Tonkin (2023). build(v) returns a model (a NamedTuple with community and n0) in which one vital rate has the value v. For each v in values the model is projected and the mean abundance of every state after burnin is recorded, averaged over replicates runs. envs is a sequence of environments, or a function r -> sequence giving the sequence for replicate r (use the same sequences for every v to remove sampling noise from the comparison).

Each state's mean abundance is then regressed on values. Returns (slope, r2, means): the slope and R² for each state (in layout order) and the matrix of mean abundances (one row per value). The slope estimates the derivative of mean abundance with respect to the vital rate; R² says how consistent the response is across the range.

MatrixCommunityModels.bumblebee — Method
bumblebee(; virus = false, parameters = BUMBLEBEE_PARAMETERS, forage = nothing,
            flowers = nothing, name = :bumblebee)

A population of bumblebee (Bombus terrestris) colonies on a daily time step; numbers can be read as per square kilometre.

Queens hibernate, emerge in spring, search for a nest site and found a nest alone; after founding_days the nest has its first workers. A colony first rears workers (growing), then males and new queens (reproducing), then stops (ending). The number of nests is limited by nest_sites. Worker, male and gyne brood take 22, 26 and 30 days. New queens mate and stay dormant, joining the hibernating queens when the season ends; everything else dies then. Colonies are pooled: workers per colony is the total number of workers over the total number of colonies.

Bumblebees keep almost no stores, so each day's intake is compared with each day's need: larvae are lost when pollen or nectar is short (gyne larvae more readily), and foragers die faster when nectar is hard to find.

With virus = true there are infected workers, infected at flowers with the daily probability given by flowers (from flower_transmission); they die infected_hazard times as fast. There is no transmission within the nest, and no mites.

forage is the result of floral_competition, if the population shares flowers.

MatrixCommunityModels.compose — Method
compose(diagram, components)

Compose open communities along an undirected wiring diagram. Box b is filled by components[b], whose pools are matched to the box's ports in order. Each junction is one pool: its total is the sum of what every attached species contributes. Outer ports of the diagram are the pools of the composite; junctions that are not exposed are shared only inside it.

MatrixCommunityModels.cypripedium_model — Method
cypripedium_model(; equivalence = true, integeronly = false, dir = joinpath(DATA_DIR, "cypripedium"))

The two-orchid community of the adapt3 community-projection vignette (Shefferson 2026): Cypripedium candidum (5 annual matrices) and C. parviflorum (17), nine stages each, cycled through in order. The species are linked by one pool, density, the number of individuals of both species, not counting dormant seed when equivalence is true. Production of dormant seed (SD) and of protocorms (P1) by flowering plants is multiplied by the Ricker terms 0.5 exp(-density) and 1.2 exp(-2 density), using RickerDensity from MatrixProjectionModels.jl. With integeronly the stage vectors are floored each step, as in adapt3.

The annual matrices were estimated in R with lefko3 and are shipped as data. Returns (community, n0, envs); the environment of step t is the integer t - 1, which selects each species' matrix for that year.

MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium — Method
equilibrium(c, n0, env; maxiter = 100_000, tol = 1e-10)

Iterate a closed community in a constant environment env from n0 until the largest change in any state, relative to max(|n|, 1), falls below tol. Returns (n, converged, iterations). A stable equilibrium is found only if the iteration converges to one; cycles and chaos return converged = false.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium_sensitivity — Method
equilibrium_sensitivity(build, θ, env; h = 1e-6, kwargs...)

Derivative of the equilibrium of a community with respect to a parameter, without simulation over a range of values. build(θ) returns a model (a NamedTuple with community and n0) for parameter value θ. The equilibrium n* is found by equilibrium (which receives kwargs), and then

dn*/dθ = (I - J)⁻¹ ∂f/∂θ

where f is one time step, J its jacobian at n*, and ∂f/∂θ is a central difference with step h * max(|θ|, 1) holding the state at n*. Returns (n, dn, converged).

This applies to a constant environment and a stable equilibrium at which the step is differentiable; thresholds and max(0, ...) terms that are active at n* break it. For fluctuating environments use abundance_sensitivity.

MatrixCommunityModels.floral_competition — Function
floral_competition(mode = :scramble; nectar, pollen, hives = 1, removal = 1,
                   overlap = 0.5, honeybee_share = 0.5, refuge = 0.2, carryover = 0.5)

Link honey bees and bumblebees through floral nectar and pollen. nectar and pollen are supply functions of the day of the year (grams of sugar and of pollen per day, see floral_supply). hives is the number of honey bee colonies drawing on the supply (per square kilometre, if bumblebees are per square kilometre), each identical to the one modelled; removal converts a gram of honey gained into grams of floral sugar removed (a colony collects about 120 kg of nectar a year, some 48 kg of sugar, which is the sugar in about 60 kg of honey: roughly 0.8 to 1).

The default supplies peak at 3 kg of sugar and 3 kg of pollen per square kilometre per day. That is of the order measured for intensively farmed land; flower-rich habitat offers 3 to 60 times as much (Hicks et al. 2016; Baude et al. 2016), with pollen and sugar in roughly equal mass.

Each species reports its demand to the pools hb_nectar, hb_pollen, bb_nectar and bb_pollen, and receives the fraction of its demand that is met. mode decides how:

  • :scramble: everyone gets the same fraction, min(1, supply / total demand).
  • :standing_crop: the supply is a stock (a flowers species, pools nectar_stock and pollen_stock) that is secreted daily and of which a fraction carryover of what is not removed persists, so heavy use one day lowers what is there the next.
  • :partitioned: a fraction overlap of the supply is open to both species; the rest is exclusive, a share honeybee_share of it to honey bees and the remainder to bumblebees.
  • :preemptive: honey bees take what they want first, except from a fraction refuge of the supply that only bumblebees can use (deep flowers); bumblebees get what is left.

Pass the result as forage to honeybee and bumblebee, and add its species (the flowers, if any) to the community.

MatrixCommunityModels.flower_transmission — Method
flower_transmission(; flowers = floral_supply(5.0e6), hives = 1, honeybee_visits = 500,
                    deposit = 0.02, bumblebee_deposit = 0.0, acquisition = 1.0)

Link honey bees and bumblebees through virus left on shared flowers. Infected foragers report their visits to the pool infectious_visits (bumblebee visits weighted by bumblebee_deposit, zero by default: bumblebees pick the virus up but do not pass it on). The fraction of flowers contaminated on a day is 1 - exp(-deposit × infectious visits / flowers(day)), so more flowers dilute the virus. A bumblebee worker is infected with daily probability 1 - exp(-acquisition × contamination); a honey bee colony's exposure is the contamination itself, which matters only if its oral_susceptibility is above zero.

None of these rates has been measured. deposit is set so that an infected forager leaves virus on about ten flowers a day (the model fitted by Burnham et al. 2021 has four a day, lasting ten days), and acquisition so that roughly a tenth of bumblebee workers carry the virus beside an untreated colony, as in field surveys (Fürst et al. 2014; Alger et al. 2019; Manley et al. 2019). Only their product matters to the bumblebees.

Pass the result as flowers to honeybee and bumblebee.

MatrixCommunityModels.honeybee — Method
honeybee(; virus = false, drones = true, parameters = HONEYBEE_PARAMETERS, forage = nothing,
           flowers = nothing, oral_susceptibility = 0.0, name = :honeybee)

The bees of a honey bee (Apis mellifera) colony on a daily time step.

The workers' life cycle is egg → larva → pupa → hive → forager, with eggs, larvae and pupae lasting 3, 5 and 12 days. The queen's laying follows hopomo_season and is capped by the brood the adults can tend; larvae are lost when nurses or pollen are short; hive bees become foragers at the rate of Khoury et al. (2013) on flying days while there is brood, so bees emerging after brood rearing stops become long-lived winter bees.

With drones = true a second life cycle, drone_egg → drone_larva → drone_pupa → drone (3, 7 and 14 days), is merged in: from late April to late August the queen lays drone_eggs drone eggs for each worker egg. Drones eat and need nursing but do no work. They matter because mites prefer, and breed better in, drone cells.

The colony's honey and pollen (grams) are two further entries of its state, added as a module: each day the stores lose what the bees eat and gain what the foragers bring in, and the share of foragers collecting pollen rises when the pollen store is low.

With virus = true the colony has three strata: healthy, carrier and infected. A larva becomes infected when an infectious mite enters its cell at capping (pool viral_phoretic, from varroa); a fraction of infected pupae die, and the adults they become die faster. A healthy in-hive bee becomes a carrier when an infectious mite takes it as a host (pools viral_phoretic and viral_emerging); as in BEEHAVE, a bee infected as an adult lives as long as a healthy one but passes the virus to mites. oral_susceptibility above zero lets adults become carriers from virus on flowers (see flower_transmission, passed as flowers).

forage is the result of floral_competition, if the colony shares flowers.

MatrixCommunityModels.jacobian — Method
jacobian(c, n, env; h = 1e-6)

Jacobian of one time step of a closed community at state n, by central differences with step h * max(|n_j|, 1). Unlike community_matrix, which freezes the pools, this includes the effect of each state on every species through the pools, so its off-diagonal blocks are the direct between-species effects. At an equilibrium, the equilibrium is locally stable if every eigenvalue has modulus below one.

MatrixCommunityModels.project — Method
project(c, n0, envs; rng = nothing)

Project a closed community from state n0 through the environments envs (one per time step). n0 is a flat vector, or species => stages pairs.

The community is lowered to a MatrixProjectionModels.CoupledMPMProblem and solved by MatrixProjectionModels.jl.

With rng (a random number generator) the projection has demographic stochasticity: individuals are whole, each survives and moves according to a multinomial draw from its column of the survival matrix, and births and inflows are Poisson with the deterministic values as means. This needs whole numbers in n0, the births of each species marked with births, and survival matrices whose columns sum to at most one.

MatrixCommunityModels.riparian_model — Method
riparian_model(; corrected = false, guilds = RIPARIAN_GUILDS, path = ...)

The five-guild riparian vegetation community of Lytle et al. (2017). Guilds are linked by one pool, space: the area occupied after the year's flood and drought mortality, in cottonwood-seedling units. Recruitment of every guild is proportional to the free space K - space.

With corrected = false the model reproduces the published R script exactly, including two behaviours that look unintended: the meadow guild receives the willow guild's recruitment (expressed here as a second pool, willow_adults), and sagebrush and meadow lose the stage-5 individuals that do not mature. corrected = true gives meadow its own recruitment, gated on its own adults, and keeps those stage-5 individuals. Both variants keep the script's floor of 10 individuals per sagebrush stage.

guilds supplies the parameters; pass a copy changed with with_rate to perturb a vital rate.

Returns (community, n0, envs, K); envs holds one environment per year of the 1916-1998 Yampa River record at Maybell.

MatrixCommunityModels.stochastic_invasion_rate — Method
stochastic_invasion_rate(invader, resident, n0, envs; burnin = 0)

Long-run growth rate of a rare invader in a fluctuating environment. The resident community is projected from n0 through envs; at each step after burnin the invader's projection matrix is evaluated at the resident's pool totals and that step's environment, and applied to the invader's stage vector, which is renormalised. Returns the geometric mean growth factor per step, so a value above one means the invader increases when rare. In a constant environment at the resident's equilibrium it equals invasion_growth_rate.

MatrixCommunityModels.varroa — Method
varroa(; virus = false, drones = true, parameters = VARROA_PARAMETERS, treatment = day -> 0.0, name = :varroa)

The mite Varroa destructor in a honey bee colony. A mite is phoretic (on an adult bee) or in a brood cell, a worker cell for 12 days or a drone_cell for 15, until the bee emerges; it then leaves with its daughters. Each day a fraction of the phoretic mites enters the cells about to be capped; the fractions depend on the pools capping, drone_capping and adult_bees reported by honeybee, and a cell takes at most four mites. Mites enter drone cells almost twelve times as readily as worker cells and leave nearly three times as many daughters there. Phoretic mites die faster while the colony has brood (pool brood). drones must be the same as in honeybee.

With virus = true mites are stratified into clean and viral. A clean mite becomes viral by entering a cell whose pupa is infected, or by taking a bee that carries the virus as its host (pools host_turnover, hive_bees, infected_hive_bees). Taking a host follows BEEHAVE: a clean mite that takes a new host, because it has just left a cell or because its host died or became a forager, becomes viral with a probability equal to the fraction of in-hive bees carrying the virus. A viral mite stays viral. Its daughters are viral if the pupa in their cell was infected and lived; if it died there are no daughters, and if it escaped infection they are clean.

treatment(day_of_year) is an extra daily mortality of phoretic mites. infection_per_cell, pupal_death and brood_survival (the chance that worker and drone brood survive the days a mite spends in the cell) repeat values of the honey bee model and default to them.
