Rivers and orchids

Fish and riparian plants of the Verde and Yampa rivers, the Cypripedium orchids, and flow regimes designed for each.

Verde River fish, riparian guilds and orchids

MatrixCommunityModels.RIPARIAN_GUILDS — Constant
RIPARIAN_GUILDS

Parameters of the five riparian guilds of Lytle et al. (2017): cottonwood C, tamarisk T, willow W, sagebrush S, meadow M. den is stem density per stage (per m²), a the maturation probabilities, S and D the flood and drought mortalities, stasis the stages with a stasis term in the original projection matrix, and area the initial area per stage (m²).

MatrixCommunityModels.cypripedium_model — Method
cypripedium_model(; equivalence = true, integeronly = false, dir = joinpath(DATA_DIR, "cypripedium"))

The two-orchid community of the adapt3 community-projection vignette (Shefferson 2026): Cypripedium candidum (5 annual matrices) and C. parviflorum (17), nine stages each, cycled through in order. The species are linked by one pool, density, the number of individuals of both species, not counting dormant seed when equivalence is true. Production of dormant seed (SD) and of protocorms (P1) by flowering plants is multiplied by the Ricker terms 0.5 exp(-density) and 1.2 exp(-2 density), using RickerDensity from MatrixProjectionModels.jl. With integeronly the stage vectors are floored each step, as in adapt3.

The annual matrices were estimated in R with lefko3 and are shipped as data. Returns (community, n0, envs); the environment of step t is the integer t - 1, which selects each species' matrix for that year.

MatrixCommunityModels.riparian_model — Method
riparian_model(; corrected = false, guilds = RIPARIAN_GUILDS, path = ...)

The five-guild riparian vegetation community of Lytle et al. (2017). Guilds are linked by one pool, space: the area occupied after the year's flood and drought mortality, in cottonwood-seedling units. Recruitment of every guild is proportional to the free space K - space.

With corrected = false the model reproduces the published R script exactly, including two behaviours that look unintended: the meadow guild receives the willow guild's recruitment (expressed here as a second pool, willow_adults), and sagebrush and meadow lose the stage-5 individuals that do not mature. corrected = true gives meadow its own recruitment, gated on its own adults, and keeps those stage-5 individuals.

The script also keeps every sagebrush stage at 10 individuals or more each year (its quasiten, described where it is defined as a rescue by sagebrush encroaching from the uplands), while its summary says sagebrush below 10 is set to zero. The original variant keeps the floor. The corrected variant treats sagebrush like the other guilds, setting stages below one individual to zero (by the package owner's decision, 2026-11-17: with the summary's rule, sagebrush is lost within seven years of the Yampa record).

guilds supplies the parameters; pass a copy changed with with_rate to perturb a vital rate.

Returns (community, n0, envs, K); envs holds one environment per year of the 1916-1998 Yampa River record at Maybell.

MatrixCommunityModels.verde_fish_model — Method
verde_fish_model(; K = 47660.0, dir = joinpath(DATA_DIR, "verde_fish"))

The seven-species Verde River fish community of Rogosch et al. (2019): three-stage biomass models linked by one pool, total community biomass, which limits every species' fecundity through max(0, 1 - biomass / K).

Returns (community, n0, envs, years, density): the composed community, the initial biomass (the stage-3 values of the vital-rate table), one environment per year of the 1964-2017 flow record (year-type flags), the years, and the individuals-per-gram conversion for each species' stages.

Designed flow regimes

MatrixCommunityModels.FLOW_INVERTEBRATES — Constant
FLOW_INVERTEBRATES

Parameters of the three invertebrates of Tonkin et al. (2021), from their code (dod-3taxa-inverts-*.R): r intrinsic rate of increase per day, h strength of flood mortality (a flood of Q m³/s leaves exp(-h Q) of the population), g rate per day at which the carrying capacity relaxes after a flood, Kd the carrying capacity just after a strong flood and Kb the baseline without floods. The mayfly Fallceon and the dragonfly Progomphus gain habitat from floods (Kd > Kb); the ostracod loses it. Qmin = 5 m³/s is the smallest flow that counts as a flood and a = 100 m³/s the half-saturation of the flood effect on carrying capacity.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.TONKIN_FISH_INITIAL — Constant
TONKIN_FISH_INITIAL

Negative binomial distributions, (size, mu), from which the authors' code draws the initial adult biomass (g) of each fish species in each of its 100 iterations (juveniles and subadults start at zero).

source
MatrixCommunityModels.TONKIN_SCENARIOS — Constant
TONKIN_SCENARIOS

The four flow regimes of Tonkin et al. (2021): :plants (the riparian prescription: a spring flood every sixth year, preceded by a drought year), :fish (a spring flood every year), :invertebrates (four small pulses a year, below the flood threshold of the plant and fish models, so that every year is a year without events for them) and :natural (the upper Verde River near Paulden, 1964-2008, repeated).

source
MatrixCommunityModels.flood_invertebrate — Method
flood_invertebrate(name; parameters = FLOW_INVERTEBRATES[name], Qmin = 5, a = 100)

One aquatic invertebrate of Tonkin et al. (2021) as a net with a single stage, N, its abundance on a scale on which the carrying capacity is at most 100, and a time step of a day. It has one transition, living, whose value depends on the day's environment (see invertebrate_flows):

  • on the first day of a flood (env.t == 1), exp(-h Q): the flood kills;
  • on any other day, the exact one-day solution of logistic growth dN/dt = r N (1 - N / K(t)), whose carrying capacity relaxes from its post-flood value K0 to Kb as K(t) = Kb + (K0 - Kb) exp(-g t), t being the day since the flood. K0 = Kpre + (Kd - Kpre) Qf(Q) moves the carrying capacity before the flood, env.Kpre[name], towards Kd by the flood effect Qf(Q) = (Q - Qmin) / (a + Q - Qmin).

Stepping a day at a time gives the closed form of McMullen et al. (2017) used in the authors' code, which solves each inter-flood interval at once.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.flood_pulses — Method
flood_pulses(every, Q, days)

Days since the last flood and peak flow for days days with a flood of Q m³/s every every days, the first on day 1: the arguments t and Q of invertebrate_flows. every is truncated to whole days, as in the authors' code (four pulses a year come every 91 days).

flow_invertebrates(; invertebrates = FLOW_INVERTEBRATES)

The mayfly, dragonfly and ostracod of Tonkin et al. (2021), each a flood_invertebrate, side by side in one community. They share no pool.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.invertebrate_flows — Method
invertebrate_flows(t, Q; invertebrates = FLOW_INVERTEBRATES, corrected = false)

One environment per day for flood_invertebrates: t is the day since the last flood (1 on the day of a flood) and Q the flood's peak flow in m³/s on Bill Williams River scale, both one value per day. Each environment holds day, t, Q and Kpre, the carrying capacity of each invertebrate just before the current flood; the first flood finds every invertebrate at its baseline Kb.

The authors' code carries the carrying capacity from one flood to the next as Kpre + (K0 - Kpre) exp(-g t), which relaxes towards the value before the flood rather than towards Kb, although within an interval the population grows under a carrying capacity that relaxes towards Kb. corrected = false (the default) reproduces the code; corrected = true carries forward the carrying capacity that the population grew under.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.tonkin_fish_start — Method
tonkin_fish_start(rng)

One draw of the initial fish biomass of the authors' code: for each species of VERDE_SPECIES, adults from the negative binomial distribution of TONKIN_FISH_INITIAL (a Poisson draw with a gamma-distributed mean), juveniles and subadults zero. Returns species => [0, 0, adults] pairs for verde_fish_model.

MatrixCommunityModels.tonkin_scenario — Method
tonkin_scenario(scenario; years = 200, invertebrate_years = 30, corrected = false)

The environments of one flow regime of Tonkin et al. (2021) (see TONKIN_SCENARIOS), for each group:

  • riparian: one per year for the community of riparian_model, with a flood, a drought or neither; seed release and drawdown are held at the values of the authors' code (cprop = 0.9, tprop = 0.4, no decline);
  • fish: one per year for verde_fish_model, with the year types of Rogosch et al. (2019);
  • invertebrates: one per day for flood_invertebrates (see invertebrate_flows).

Returns (riparian, fish, invertebrates, riparian_n0, invertebrate_n0): the riparian start is 3978 m² of every stage of every guild, the invertebrates start at 50. Peak flows of the natural regime are the mean spring and autumn peaks at Paulden (1134 and 459 cfs), scaled by 0.35 to m³/s on the Bill Williams River, on which the invertebrate models were parameterised. The factor is not a unit conversion (1 m³/s is about 35 cfs) but the authors' match between corresponding floods on the two rivers: a bankfull flood is 220 cfs on the Verde and 87 m³/s on the Bill Williams, a large flood 700 cfs and 215 m³/s, and 0.35 lies between the two ratios. The designed floods use it too (700 * 0.35).
