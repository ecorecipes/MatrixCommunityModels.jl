Composing and projecting communities

Species as open communities, their composition along undirected wiring diagrams, the state of a community, its projection, and freezing it into ordinary matrices.

Open communities and their composition

These are defined in CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl and re-exported by this package.

CategoricalPopulationDynamics.OpenCommunity — Type
OpenCommunity

One or more species with exposed pools (its ports). Build one species with species, and join them with community or compose.

The flattened model is a PooledProjectionSystem, available from system. The state of a community is a flat vector: one block of stages per species, in the order of species_names (see state_vector). length(c) is the number of states, and c[name] the responsive net of a species, with its responses reading the pools of the composite.

source
CategoricalPopulationDynamics.species — Function
species(name, net; occupies = [], reads = [])

A single species as an OpenCommunity. net is a valued or responsive projection net. occupies lists pool => contribution pairs saying how much of each pool the species occupies; a contribution is a vector of weights (one per stage), a list of stage => weight pairs, a number (weight on every stage), or a function (n, env) -> number. reads names pools that the species' responses read without contributing to them.

grass = species(:grass, respond(life, :seeding => ctx -> max(0, 1 - ctx.pool.space / 1000));
                occupies = [:space => [:seedling => 0.1, :adult => 1.0]])

A pool can also hold one value for each of a set of classes, such as the prey on offer to predators of each size class: a contribution is then a matrix of weights, one row per class and one column per stage, or a function (n, env) -> vector, and a response reads the pool as ctx.pool.name[i]. Every contribution to such a pool is a vector of the same length (see Pool), and pool_totals gives the pool as a vector.

CategoricalPopulationDynamics.community — Function
community(components...)

Compose open communities that share pools by name: every pool named by any component becomes one junction, exposed as a pool of the result. The wiring diagram that was generated is available from diagram.

source
AlgebraicInterfaces.compose — Method
compose(diagram, components)

Compose open communities along an undirected wiring diagram. Box b is filled by components[b], whose pools are matched to the box's ports in order. Each junction is one pool: its total is the sum of what every attached species contributes. Outer ports of the diagram are the pools of the composite; junctions that are not exposed are shared only inside it.

A junction is named by its variable in the diagram (as in a diagram written with @relation), or else by the first pool attached to it. A species' responses keep reading their pools under the names the species gave them. Composites nest: a component may itself be a composite.

This is a method of Catlab's compose.

CategoricalPopulationDynamics.advance — Method
advance(c, n, env; rng = nothing)

Advance a closed community one time step from state n (a flat vector, or species => stages pairs), returning the new flat state: advance of its system.

With rng (a random number generator), the step has demographic stochasticity: individuals are whole, each survives and moves according to a multinomial draw from its column of the survival matrix, and births and inflows are Poisson with the deterministic values as means. This needs whole numbers in n, the births of each species marked with births, and survival matrices whose columns sum to at most one. The numbers are drawn for each species in turn: survival, births, inflows.

Projection and frozen matrices

MatrixCommunityModels.MatrixCommunityModels — Module
MatrixCommunityModels

Matrix community models (Lytle & Tonkin 2023) built from the ecorecipes categorical stack.

A species is a projection net from CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl: named stages, named transitions, and responses saying how a transition's values depend on shared pools and the environment. A community is a composite of species along an undirected wiring diagram whose junctions are the pools (space, biomass, parasites per host, ...). The composite is a PooledProjectionSystem, which is lowered to a coupled problem: a CoupledIPMProblem solved by IntegralProjectionModels.jl when a species has a continuous stage (an integral projection model), a CoupledMPMProblem solved by MatrixProjectionModels.jl otherwise.

The open communities and their composition along wiring diagrams (species, community, compose, ...) are those of CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl, re-exported here. This package adds their projection, the analysis of the composed community, continuous stages and kernels, and a set of published models.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.CommunityTrajectory — Type
CommunityTrajectory

Result of project. u[t + 1, :] is the state at time t; pools[t + 1, :] holds the totals of the exposed pools used for the step from t. Index with a species name to get its stage-by-time matrix.

A pool with a value for each of a set of classes (see species) has NaN in pools; its values are in pool_vectors[name][t + 1, :], one column per class. The last row of both, for the final state, is NaN.

MatrixCommunityModels.ipm_problem — Method
ipm_problem(c, species, n, env; tspan = (0, 1))

The IntegralProjectionModels.IPMProblem of species frozen in community state n and environment env. Its kernel is an IntegralProjectionModels.DiscretizedKernel: the species' projection matrix at that moment, with survival and births kept apart, laid out over its stages, a continuous stage (see ContinuousStage) as one continuous state and every other stage as a discrete state of its own. Solve it with IntegralProjectionModels.jl, for example solve(ipm_problem(c, :plant, n, env), EigenAnalysis()) for the growth rate, stable state and reproductive values of the species in that state. The starting population of the problem is the species' block of n.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.matrix_model — Method
matrix_model(c, species, n, env)

The MatrixProjectionModels.MatrixProjectionModel of species frozen in community state n and environment env, with births (see births) kept apart from survival, so that the single-species analyses of MatrixProjectionModels.jl apply.

MatrixCommunityModels.matrix_net — Method
matrix_net(stages, name => matrix, ...)

A ValuedProjectionNet whose transitions are given as whole matrices (rows are destinations, columns are sources): every non-zero entry of matrix becomes an entry of the transition name. Use it for a discretised integral projection model, whose kernels arrive as matrices.

MatrixCommunityModels.project — Method
project(c, n0, envs; rng = nothing, solver = :auto)

Project a closed community from state n0 through the environments envs (one per time step). n0 is a flat vector, or species => stages pairs.

The community is lowered by CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl to a coupled problem and solved by one of the model packages of the stack, chosen by solver:

  • :auto (the default): a community with a continuous stage (a species built with kernel_net) is an integral projection model, lowered to a IntegralProjectionModels.CoupledIPMProblem and solved by IntegralProjectionModels.jl; a species without one is a component whose classes are all discrete. Any other community is lowered to a MatrixProjectionModels.CoupledMPMProblem and solved by MatrixProjectionModels.jl.
  • :ipm or :mpm: the one or the other, whatever the stages.

Both step the same model in the same way, so they give the same trajectory.

With rng (a random number generator) the projection has demographic stochasticity: individuals are whole, each survives and moves according to a multinomial draw from its column of the survival matrix, and births and inflows are Poisson with the deterministic values as means. This needs whole numbers in n0, the births of each species marked with births, and survival matrices whose columns sum to at most one. A community solved by IntegralProjectionModels.jl is then solved in the demographic mode of its coupled problem; the others are stepped here. Both draw the numbers in the same order (for each species in turn: survival, births, inflows), so a generator in a given state gives the same projection whichever solver is used.

Recruitment into a limited resource

A lottery in which the recruits of a time step share the free space.

MatrixCommunityModels.lottery — Method
lottery(net, transitions...; capacity, space = :space, demand = :demand, rule = :lottery)

Make the named recruitment transitions of net compete for a limited resource, such as space, within each time step. The individuals passing through these transitions in a step (the demand) share the free space, capacity less the space held: each establishes with probability min(1, free / demand) (rule = :lottery: every recruit has the same chance, and no more establish than there is room for) or free / (free + demand) (rule = :saturating: recruits and free sites meet at random, and the number established, demand × free / (free + demand), rises smoothly towards the free space). rule can also be a function (free, demand) -> probability. With no demand the probability is one if there is free space and zero otherwise; a negative free space counts as none. Returns a responsive net with this response attached to each of transitions.

This is the device for pulse recruitment into a finite resource. With synchronous updates, recruitment limited only by the free space at the start of a step is not limited within the step, and a pulse of recruits can overshoot the space; the lottery cannot.

  • space: where the space held is read. A pool name (the space held by every species attached to that pool), or the species' own stages that hold space, as stage names or stage => weight pairs (the species competes only with itself).
  • demand: the recruits competing in a step. A pool name: the recruits of every species attached to that pool, each of which contributes its own with lottery_demand in species. Or :own: the species' own recruits, computed from the transitions' values in the step.

In both cases the demand counts, for each entry of transitions, its value with the transition's other responses (those attached before lottery) evaluated, times the abundance of its source stage. Attach lottery after expand_stage or stratify, since it refers to the net's stages as they are.

Two annual plants sharing space by lottery, as in the seasons vignette (germinating seeds leave the seed bank and establish if they win a site):

plant = respond(life, :germination => ctx -> ctx.env == germinate ? 1.0 : 0.0)
plant = lottery(plant, :germination; capacity = 1000.0, space = :space, demand = :demand)
species(name, plant; occupies = [:space => [:plant => 1.0], :demand => lottery_demand(plant, :germination)])

A weed whose seedlings compete for the sites its own rosettes leave open:

lottery(net, :emerging, :establishing; capacity = sites, space = [:small, :medium, :large],
        demand = :own, rule = :saturating)
source
MatrixCommunityModels.lottery_demand — Method
lottery_demand(net, transitions...)

A species' contribution to the demand pool of a lottery: a function (n, env) -> number giving the individuals that pass through the named transitions of net in a step, for use in occupies of species. Each entry of the transitions counts its value, with the transition's responses evaluated (except lotteries), times the abundance of its source stage. A contribution sees only the stages and the environment, so a response on these transitions that reads a pool is an error; use demand = :own in lottery for a species that competes only with itself.
