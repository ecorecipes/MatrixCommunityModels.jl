Resilience metrics

Composed communities in the terms of ResilienceCore.jl, whose metrics dispatch on what a matrix means:

  • linearise gives the Jacobian of one step (or of a cycle) at a fixed point as a DiscreteJacobian, for isstable, resilience, reactivity, max_amplification, the median metrics and the press sensitivities;
  • periodic_operator gives the matrices of a rare invader over a cycle of environments as a PeriodicOperator, for its growth_rate and resilience per step;
  • MCMState gives a community at a fixed point the community interface, so that ResilienceCore's simulated experiments (press_response, removal_responses, global_mortality_tolerance, local_mortality_tolerances, extinction_tolerance) run on it.

A mortality press of strength f can mean two things, chosen with MCMState(...; press): press = :hazard (the default) multiplies every stage's mortality hazard by f (survival σ → σ^f), and press = :removal removes a fraction m = removal_scale × (f - 1) of every stage after each step (n ← (1 - m) advance(n)), an added loss rather than a multiple of the baseline mortality. Both are the model itself at f = 1. The hazard press falls hardest on stages that almost all die (a 10% press halves the survival of riparian cottonwood seedlings, which survive a year with probability about 0.0009), the removal press equally on every stage; see the resilience tutorial.

jacobian and linearise are ResilienceCore's functions with methods added here, so the two packages can be loaded together. EcologicalResilience.jl, which also builds on ResilienceCore, exports some names that differ from this package's, such as equilibrium and subcommunity; with both loaded, qualify them (MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium). (At present the two cannot be installed in one environment: MatrixProjectionModels.jl requires SciMLBase 2 and EcologicalResilience.jl SciMLBase 3.)

MatrixCommunityModels.MCMState — Type
MCMState(c, n, env; press = :hazard, removal_scale = 1.0, settle = true, newton = true,
         extinct = 1e-6, tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 100_000)

The closed community c at state n in the constant environment env, with the community interface of ResilienceCore.jl. ResilienceCore's simulated experiments then run unchanged: press_response, removal_responses, global_mortality_tolerance, local_mortality_tolerances and extinction_tolerance.

  • Species and abundance. Species are those of c, in the order of species_names. state(s) is each species' abundance, the sum over its states (as in abundance); a species is present while that sum exceeds extinct.
  • Mortality presses. with_mortality(s, factors) applies a press of strength factors[i] to species i; factors[i] = 1 is the model itself. What a press does is chosen by press:
    • press = :hazard (the default) multiplies the mortality hazard of every stage by the factor f: a stage that survives a step with probability σ survives with probability σ^f. It acts on the species' survival transitions (those of survival_fecundity_matrices) and leaves births alone. This is "baseline mortality × f" with mortality read as a hazard, which keeps a small press small for stages that almost all die, such as seedlings, and hits them hardest in relative terms.
    • press = :removal removes a fixed fraction of every stage after each step, newborns included, as harvesting or culling would: n ← (1 - m) advance(n) for the states of species i, with m = min(removal_scale[i] * (f - 1), 1). With the default removal_scale = 1, f = 1.1 removes 10% of the species each step. This is an added loss proportional to f - 1, not baseline mortality multiplied by f: it ignores how many die anyway, and it does not depend on the stage. removal_scale (a number, or one per species) converts the factors of ResilienceCore's experiments into fractions removed. Factors below one would add individuals and are an error.
  • Removal. without_species(s, i) sets the states of species i to zero and holds them there.
  • Steady state. After a perturbation, and on construction when settle = true, the community is iterated in env to a fixed point, trying Newton's method first when newton = true; species whose abundance falls below extinct are removed. If no fixed point is reached within maxiter steps (cycles, chaos), the state reached is kept and s.converged is false; on construction this is an error. The keyword steady of the experiments may be a NamedTuple overriding tol and maxiter.
  • Local stability. jacobian(s) is the Jacobian of one (perturbed) step over the states of the species present, and linearise(s) the corresponding DiscreteJacobian.

The state of every stage is s.n, in the layout of c.

Loading both this package and EcologicalResilience.jl leaves names that differ between them, such as equilibrium and subcommunity: qualify them, as in MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium.

MatrixCommunityModels.periodic_operator — Method
periodic_operator(invader, pool, envs)

The projection matrices of a rare invader (a single species, from species) over one cycle of environments envs, with the pools held at pool (a NamedTuple), as a ResilienceCore PeriodicOperator whose parts are applied in the order of envs. Its monodromy is periodic_matrix(invader, pool, envs), so its growth_rate is the invader's growth rate per step (the per-cycle rate to the power 1/length(envs)) and its resilience the rate at which its stage structure settles, per step. Continuous stages carry their mesh weights.

ResilienceCore.linearise — Method
linearise(c, n, env; period = 1, check = true, tol = 1e-8, h = 1e-6)

The Jacobian of the closed community c at a fixed point n of its dynamics in the constant environment env, as a ResilienceCore DiscreteJacobian whose equilibrium is n. The metrics of ResilienceCore.jl for discrete time then apply: isstable, resilience, reactivity, max_amplification, the median metrics and the press sensitivities.

With period = k, n is a point of a k-cycle and the result is the Jacobian of the k-step map: the product of the one-step jacobians around the cycle. Its eigenvalues are the multipliers that periodic_point returns, and its metrics describe one pass around the cycle.

check verifies that n returns to itself after period steps, to the relative tolerance tol; find the point first with periodic_point (which also finds unstable points) or equilibrium. h is the relative step of the central differences.
