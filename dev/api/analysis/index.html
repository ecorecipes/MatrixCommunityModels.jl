MatrixCommunityModels.InteractionNetwork — Type
InteractionNetwork(effect, labels; threshold)

A directed network read off a matrix of net effects, such as the slopes from abundance_sensitivity: effect[i, j] is the effect of perturbing node j on node i, and there is a link j → i when |effect[i, j]| > threshold. Self-effects are not links. Use links, connectance, link_density, reciprocity, out_degree and in_degree.

MatrixCommunityModels.abundance_sensitivity — Method
abundance_sensitivity(build, values, envs; burnin = 0, replicates = 1)

The simulation-based sensitivity of Lytle & Tonkin (2023). build(v) returns a model (a NamedTuple with community and n0) in which one vital rate has the value v. For each v in values the model is projected and the mean abundance of every state after burnin is recorded, averaged over replicates runs. envs is a sequence of environments, or a function r -> sequence giving the sequence for replicate r (use the same sequences for every v to remove sampling noise from the comparison).

Each state's mean abundance is then regressed on values. Returns (slope, r2, means): the slope and R² for each state (in layout order) and the matrix of mean abundances (one row per value). The slope estimates the derivative of mean abundance with respect to the vital rate; R² says how consistent the response is across the range.

MatrixCommunityModels.ensemble — Method
ensemble(c, n0, envs; replicates, rng, demographic = true)

Project a closed community replicates times and collect the results in a CommunityEnsemble. envs is a sequence of environments used for every replicate, or a function r -> sequence giving replicate r its own (environmental stochasticity). With demographic = true each projection has demographic stochasticity drawn from rng (see project); with false, rng is not used and replicates differ only through envs.

MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium — Method
equilibrium(c, n0, env; maxiter = 100_000, tol = 1e-10)

Iterate a closed community in a constant environment env from n0 until the largest change in any state, relative to max(|n|, 1), falls below tol. Returns (n, converged, iterations). A stable equilibrium is found only if the iteration converges to one; cycles and chaos return converged = false.

MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium — Method
equilibrium(species, pool => (low, high), n0, env; tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 200)

The equilibrium of a single species that regulates itself through one pool, found without iterating the dynamics: the value of pool between low and high at which the species, with its matrix evaluated there, has a growth rate of one. The state returned is the stable distribution of that matrix, scaled so that the species' contribution to the pool equals the value found. Returns (n, total, converged, iterations), where total is the pool value.

Use it when iteration is slow, for example a long-lived species on a large mesh, whose approach to equilibrium takes thousands of steps. It assumes that the matrix depends on the state only through the pool (and through totals that scale with it), that the species' contribution to the pool is proportional to its abundance, and that the growth rate falls below one somewhere between low and high and exceeds it elsewhere in that range. n0 is any state of the species with a positive contribution to the pool; only its shape is used, to evaluate the matrix. The equilibrium found need not be stable: check with jacobian, or by projecting from it.

MatrixCommunityModels.equilibrium_sensitivity — Method
equilibrium_sensitivity(build, θ, env; h = 1e-6, kwargs...)

Derivative of the equilibrium of a community with respect to a parameter, without simulation over a range of values. build(θ) returns a model (a NamedTuple with community and n0) for parameter value θ. The equilibrium n* is found by equilibrium (which receives kwargs), and then

dn*/dθ = (I - J)⁻¹ ∂f/∂θ

where f is one time step, J its jacobian at n*, and ∂f/∂θ is a central difference with step h * max(|θ|, 1) holding the state at n*. Returns (n, dn, converged).

This applies to a constant environment and a stable equilibrium at which the step is differentiable; thresholds and max(0, ...) terms that are active at n* break it. For fluctuating environments use abundance_sensitivity.

MatrixCommunityModels.invasion_growth_rate — Method
invasion_growth_rate(invader, pool, env)
invasion_growth_rate(invader, resident, n, env)

Asymptotic growth rate of a rare invader (a single species, from species) when the pools are held at the totals pool (a NamedTuple), or at the totals of the resident community in state n. A value above one means the invader increases when rare. The invader's own contribution to the pools is neglected, as is any dependence of its rates on its own abundance.

MatrixCommunityModels.invasion_sensitivity — Method
invasion_sensitivity(invader, pool, env)
invasion_sensitivity(invader, resident, n, env)

Sensitivity and elasticity of the invasion_growth_rate λ of a rare invader to its own vital rates, with the pools held at the totals pool (a NamedTuple) or at the totals of the resident community in state n. It answers the question: which change to an excluded species (λ < 1) would let it persist?

They follow from the invader's matrix A at those pools by the eigenvector formula ∂λ/∂a_ij = v_i w_j / ⟨v, w⟩, with w and v the right and left eigenvectors of λ. The pools are held fixed: a small change to a rare invader does not change the residents. Returns a NamedTuple with

  • lambda: the invasion growth rate;
  • sensitivity and elasticity: matrices over the invader's stages (rows are destinations, columns sources), ∂λ/∂a_ij and (a_ij/λ) ∂λ/∂a_ij; the elasticities sum to one;
  • transitions: one row per named transition, (transition, sensitivity, elasticity), where sensitivity is ∂λ/∂m for a factor m multiplying every value of the transition (at m = 1), that is the sum of a_ij ∂λ/∂a_ij over its entries, and elasticity is sensitivity / λ, the proportional change in λ per proportional change in the transition;
  • entries: one row per entry of each transition, (transition, from, to, value, sensitivity, elasticity), with value the entry evaluated at the pools, sensitivity = ∂λ/∂a_ij and elasticity = (value/λ) ∂λ/∂a_ij.

Two transitions that share an entry each get its contribution in full. For the sensitivity to a parameter that enters several rates, or the residents too, use the method below.

invasion_sensitivity(build, θ; h = 1e-6)

Derivative of the invasion growth rate with respect to a parameter θ, by central differences with step h * max(|θ|, 1). build(θ) returns a NamedTuple, either (invader, pool, env) or (invader, resident, n, env); whether the residents' state n is recomputed for each θ (for example by equilibrium) is up to build. Returns (lambda, derivative, elasticity) with elasticity = θ/λ × derivative.

MatrixCommunityModels.periodic_matrix — Method
periodic_matrix(species, pool, envs)

The projection matrix of species over one cycle of environments envs (for example the seasons of a year) with the pools held at pool (a NamedTuple): the product A(envs[end]) ⋯ A(envs[1]). Its dominant eigenvalue is the growth factor per cycle, so with pool describing an empty community it gives the species' annual growth rate when rare.

MatrixCommunityModels.periodic_point — Method
periodic_point(c, n0, env; period = 1, tol = 1e-12, maxiter = 100)

A point of a closed community that returns to itself after period steps in the constant environment env, found by Newton's method from n0: an equilibrium for period = 1, a point of a two-cycle for period = 2. Unlike equilibrium, which iterates the dynamics, this also finds unstable equilibria and cycles. Returns (n, converged, iterations, multipliers); multipliers are the eigenvalues of the Jacobian of the period-step map at n (the product of the one-step Jacobians around the cycle), so the point is locally stable if all have modulus below one. If Newton's method leaves the region where the model is finite, it stops with converged = false.

MatrixCommunityModels.stochastic_invasion_rate — Method
stochastic_invasion_rate(invader, resident, n0, envs; burnin = 0)

Long-run growth rate of a rare invader in a fluctuating environment. The resident community is projected from n0 through envs; at each step after burnin the invader's projection matrix is evaluated at the resident's pool totals and that step's environment, and applied to the invader's stage vector, which is renormalised. Returns the geometric mean growth factor per step, so a value above one means the invader increases when rare. In a constant environment at the resident's equilibrium it equals invasion_growth_rate.

source
ResilienceCore.jacobian — Method
jacobian(c, n, env; h = 1e-6)

Jacobian of one time step of a closed community at state n, by central differences with step h * max(|n_j|, 1). Unlike community_matrix, which freezes the pools, this includes the effect of each state on every species through the pools, so its off-diagonal blocks are the direct between-species effects. At an equilibrium, the equilibrium is locally stable if every eigenvalue has modulus below one.
