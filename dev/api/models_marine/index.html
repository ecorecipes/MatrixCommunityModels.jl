Reefs and seas

Sea fans, corals and size-structured fish communities.

Sea fans and aspergillosis

MatrixCommunityModels.SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS — Constant
SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS

Default parameters of sea_fan: the integral projection model of the sea fan Gorgonia ventalina of Bruno, Ellner, Vu, Kim & Harvell (2011), fitted to about 750 colonies followed from 2002 to 2005 on five reefs, two in the Florida Keys and three off the Yucatán. Size is the cube root of the area of healthy tissue in cm², and the time step is a year. The rates that changed over the epizootic are in SEA_FAN_PHASES. Sources of the values are in the comments of src/seafans.jl.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.SEA_FAN_PHASES — Constant
SEA_FAN_PHASES

The three phases of the aspergillosis epizootic in sea_fan, after Bruno et al. (2011): pre_epizootic (before 1994), peak (the outbreak, fitted to surveys of 1997 to 1999 in the Florida Keys) and endemic (after the peak, from colonies followed in the Florida Keys and the Yucatán from 2002 to 2005). A phase is the environment of a time step, so an epizootic is a sequence of phases:

reef = sea_fan()
project(reef, n0, [fill(SEA_FAN_PHASES.peak, 6); fill(SEA_FAN_PHASES.endemic, 8)])

Each phase holds

  • small_fan_risk, large_fan_risk, risk_per_cm: the chance that a healthy fan is infected within the year. It is small_fan_risk up to the size large_fan of SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS, and above it large_fan_risk plus risk_per_cm for each cm of height above risk_height, which is 20 cm;
  • recovery: the chance that an infected fan that survives the year is healthy at its end;
  • disease_mortality: mortality of infected fans of any size, added to the mortality that depends on size;
  • recruitment: a multiplier of the recruits produced, which were far fewer before 2003.

A phase is a NamedTuple, so a variant is made with merge, for example merge(SEA_FAN_PHASES.endemic, (recovery = 0.2,)).

MatrixCommunityModels.sea_fan — Method
sea_fan(; parameters = SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS, cells = 100, resistant = false,
          outside = 1.0, transmission = 0.0, name = :sea_fan)

The sea fan Gorgonia ventalina with aspergillosis, as the integral projection model of Bruno et al. (2011): colonies are classified by size (see sea_fan_stage) and by infection status. Numbers are colonies per m² of suitable habitat and the time step is a year.

The life cycle is that of a healthy fan, which survives and grows (growing) and, if 20 cm or taller, produces recruits (recruiting), with the kernels of an infected fan that survives beside it: one that recovers (recovering) and one that stays infected (lingering). stratify makes the strata healthy and infected: a healthy fan that grows stays healthy or is infected, and recovering leads back, so the stages are fan1_healthy, ..., fan1_infected, ... and the transitions growing_healthy, growing_healthy_to_infected, recovering_infected_to_healthy, lingering_infected and recruiting_healthy. The chance of each fate at each size is then attached to the transitions that leave one stratum, with by_trait(...; stratum = ...).

The environment of a time step is the phase of the epizootic, one of SEA_FAN_PHASES or a variant of one; with nothing the phase is endemic.

  • resistant = true adds a third stratum, resistant, of fans that cannot be infected and whose recruits are resistant too: they have the life cycle of a healthy fan (growing_resistant, recruiting_resistant) and are never infected. resistant = :only gives resistant fans alone, to be composed with the others as a species of their own.
  • The species adds its healthy tissue, in cm², to the pool tissue, and that of its infected fans to the pool infectious.
  • outside and transmission say where the infection comes from. The chance of infection given by the phase is multiplied by outside + transmission * infectious / tissue, read from the pools. With the defaults all infection comes from outside the reef and the model is the published one, which is linear. transmission > 0 makes a share of it depend on the share of tissue that is infected; the paper has no such term and gives no value.

MatrixCommunityModels.sea_fan_stage — Method
sea_fan_stage(; parameters = SEA_FAN_PARAMETERS, cells = 100)

The ContinuousStage from which sea_fan is built: colonies by size, the cube root of the area of healthy tissue in cm². meshpoints of it are the sizes of the cells. In the stratified net its cell fan7 becomes fan7_healthy and fan7_infected; ctx.n[stage] gives the numbers by size whatever the stratum, and ctx.n[stage, :infected] those of the infected fans.

Corals of Mo'orea

MatrixCommunityModels.CORAL_PARAMETERS — Constant
CORAL_PARAMETERS

Demographic parameters of three coral genera on four fore reefs of Mo'orea, French Polynesia, for coral: Table S1 of Kayal et al. (2018), estimated from colonies followed in 2011-2013 (survival and growth, by generalised linear mixed models) and from recruits counted in 2011-2012 (the intercept of recruitment); the slopes of recruitment come from an earlier study (Kayal et al. 2015).

CORAL_PARAMETERS[reef][taxon], with reef one of :lter1, :lter2 (north shore), :lter4 (east), :lter5 (west) and taxon one of :acropora, :pocillopora, :porites, holds:

  • survival: intercept and slope of the logit of survival in size z;
  • growth: intercept and slope of the mean size next year, and its standard deviation sd;
  • recruitment: intercept R0 and slope Rγ of log recruitment, and crowding, the quantity γ it responds to (:own_density, the colonies of the taxon itself, for Acropora and Porites; :assemblage_log_size, the summed sizes of all colonies of the three taxa, for Pocillopora).

Size z is log10(S + 1), with S the surface area of a colony's living tissue in cm².

source
MatrixCommunityModels.CORAL_RECRUITMENT_CAP — Constant
CORAL_RECRUITMENT_CAP

The largest number of recruits per 10 m² in a year, 348, the highest recruitment measured for a population by Kayal et al. (2018, Appendix S3), who capped recruitment there.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.coral — Method
coral(taxon; reef = :lter1, parameters = CORAL_PARAMETERS, reading = :as_written,
      recruit_size = (0.6, 0.2), cap = CORAL_RECRUITMENT_CAP, cells = 100, name = taxon)

A coral genus (:acropora, :pocillopora or :porites) on one fore reef of Mo'orea (:lter1, :lter2, :lter4, :lter5) as the open-population integral projection model of Kayal et al. (2018), with the parameters of CORAL_PARAMETERS. Colonies are structured by size z = log10(S + 1) (coral_stage); numbers are colonies per 10 m² and the time step is a year.

The net has one transition, growing: a colony of size z survives with probability logit⁻¹(s₀ + sz z) and grows to a size that is normal with mean G₀ + Gz z and standard deviation GSD. Recruits are not born of the local colonies but settle from the plankton: each year [`coralrecruits](@ref) of them arrive, a number set by crowding, with sizes normal with mean and standard deviationrecruit_size` (0.6 and 0.2 in the initial runs of the paper; 1.2 and 0.4 for Acropora in its later runs). They are added as inflows to the cells of the stage.

The species adds its colonies and their summed sizes z to the pools <taxon>_colonies and <taxon>_log_size. Acropora and Porites read their own colonies; Pocillopora reads the summed sizes of all three taxa (acropora_log_size, pocillopora_log_size, porites_log_size), which a community of the three supplies (see coral_community). The environment of a step is not used.

MatrixCommunityModels.coral_observed_state — Method
coral_observed_state(reef, year)

The coral colonies observed on the fore reef reef (:lter1, :lter2, :lter4, :lter5) of Mo'orea in year (2013 or 2015), binned onto the mesh of coral_stage (100 cells), in colonies per 10 m² in each cell: a vector of taxon => stages pairs, one per genus (:acropora, :pocillopora, :porites), which project and state_vector accept as the state of a coral_community of the default 100 cells. Kayal et al. (2018) started their updated runs from these states (Fig. 4: from 2013 to 2015, and from 2015 to 2020):

c = coral_community(:lter1; recruit_size = (acropora = (1.2, 0.4), pocillopora = (0.6, 0.2), porites = (0.6, 0.2)))
sol = project(c, coral_observed_state(:lter1, 2015), fill(nothing, 5))   # 2020 in the last row

The colonies are those mapped in four permanent 5 m × 1 m transects per reef by the Mo'orea Coral Reef LTER (Lenihan et al., EDI data package knb-lter-mcr.4009, revision 5, CC BY 4.0; see data/corals/LICENSE.txt). Acropora was mapped over the whole of each transect (20 m² per reef), Pocillopora and Porites over its first metre (4 m²), and the counts are scaled to 10 m² accordingly. A colony's size is z = log10(S + 1), with its surface area S estimated from its length, width and height as in Appendix S2 of the paper (a sphere for Pocillopora, a cylinder for the other two).

source
MatrixCommunityModels.coral_observed_transects — Method
coral_observed_transects(reef, year)

The colonies of coral_observed_state totalled by transect: for each genus (:acropora, :pocillopora, :porites), a NamedTuple of colonies (colonies per 10 m²) and log_size (summed sizes z per 10 m²), each a vector with one entry for each of the four transects mapped on reef in year (2013 or 2015). Their means are the observed abundances of Figs. S2, S5 and S7 of Kayal et al. (2018), and their standard errors those behind the figures' confidence intervals and z-scores.

MatrixCommunityModels.coral_recruits — Method
coral_recruits(taxon, γ; reef = :lter1, parameters = CORAL_PARAMETERS,
               reading = :as_written, cap = CORAL_RECRUITMENT_CAP)

Recruits per 10 m² in a year of taxon on reef, when its crowding is γ per 10 m² (colonies of the taxon itself for Acropora and Porites, summed sizes z of the colonies of all three taxa for Pocillopora). The recruitment function of Kayal et al. (2018, Appendix S3) is log r = R0 + Rγ γ; reading says how its units are read:

  • :as_written (the default): the natural logarithm, as in the authors' R script, with r and γ per 10 m². Read this way, Acropora and Porites hold far more of the recovering assemblage than the paper's figures show.
  • :calibrated: the logarithm is to base 10, R0 is for the 25 m² plots in which recruits were counted (so r = 0.4 × 10^(R0 + Rγ γ/10) per 10 m²), and γ is per m². This reading reproduces the compositions and densities published for the simulations (Fig. 3, Fig. S2 and Fig. S4 of the paper); it is a reconstruction (see the vignette).

Either is capped at cap.

The ContinuousStage of coral: colonies by size z = log10(S + 1), S the surface area of living tissue in cm², from 0 to 7 in cells cells (100 in Kayal et al. 2018, Appendix S3).

North Sea fish by length (LeMans)

MatrixCommunityModels.LEMANS_NORTH_SEA — Constant
LEMANS_NORTH_SEA

The North Sea data of the LeMans model for lemans_parameters: the 21 species of Thorpe et al. (2015), as shipped with the R package LeMaRns 0.1.2 (Spence et al. 2020; GPL-3) and read from data/lemans. A NamedTuple of vectors, one entry per species:

  • species (symbols such as :cod, :poor_cod) and names ("Cod", "Poor cod");
  • Linf (asymptotic length, cm) and k (per year), the von Bertalanffy growth curve;
  • W_a, W_b: weight in g is W_a L^W_b for length L in cm;
  • Lmat: the length (cm) at which half the fish are mature;
  • a, b: hockey-stick recruitment, min(a S, b) million recruits a year for a spawning stock biomass of S thousand tonnes (LeMaRns's NS_par);
  • gear, max_catchability, eta, L50: the fleet (:industrial, :otter, :beam or :pelagic) that catches each species, with a logistic selectivity of slope eta per cm and half-selection length L50 cm, as in LeMaRns's mixed-fishery data (NS_mixed_fish, NS_eta, NS_L50);

and two entries for the community: diet, a matrix whose entry [i, j] is true when species i eats species j (NS_tau), and other, the biomass in g of the food outside the model (NS_other).

MatrixCommunityModels.lemans_fish — Method
lemans_fish(p, species)

One species of the LeMans model with parameters p (see lemans_parameters), as an OpenCommunity that meets the others through two pools with a value for each predator class of the community (p.predators):

  • ration: the food in g that the predators of each class need in a step, ration × N;
  • prey: the prey biomass in g that the predators of each class find suitable, Σ suitability × weight × N over the length classes of every species.

The species adds its own fish to both and reads both. Its stages are its length classes, the cells of p.fish[species].stage (length1, length2, ...), and it has three transitions:

  • staying: the fish of a class that survive the step and stay in their class;
  • growing: those that survive and grow into the next class, a share leaving of each;
  • spawning: the recruits, which join the smallest class at the first step of a year, then die and grow as the fish of that class do in the same step. Marked as births.

The share that survives a step is exp(-Z), with Z the sum of the background mortality natural, the fishing mortality effort × catchability × dt, the predation mortality

M2 = suitabilityᵀ (pool.ration ./ (pool.prey .+ p.other))

and p.eps. Recruitment is min(a S / 10⁹, b) × 10⁶ fish for a spawning stock biomass of S g (Σ spawners × N), shared among the spawners in proportion to their weight. The recruits are prey in the step they join, so they count in the pool prey of that step.

The environment of a step is a NamedTuple with recruiting (whether this is the first step of a year) and effort (a NamedTuple of fishing effort by fleet, missing fleets not fishing); lemans_environment makes the sequence for a run.

MatrixCommunityModels.lemans_parameters — Function
lemans_parameters(data = LEMANS_NORTH_SEA; classes = 32, dt = 0.1, pred_mu = -2.25,
                  pred_sigma = 0.5, other = data.other, kappa = 10.0, Nmort = 0.8,
                  prop = 0.75, growth_eff = 0.5, growth_eff_decay = 0.11, eps = 1e-5,
                  selectivity_at = :upper)

The quantities of the LeMans model derived from the species' data, as LeMaRns's LeMansParam derives them with its defaults (hockey-stick recruitment, growth efficiency falling with length, background mortality "std_RNM"). A NamedTuple with

  • species, names, gears and dt (the time step in years);
  • lower, upper, mid: the bounds and midpoints (cm) of the classes length classes, of equal width from 0 to 1.01 times the largest Linf;
  • fish: one NamedTuple for each species (p.fish.cod), with its ContinuousStage stage, whose cells are its length classes up to the one that holds its Linf, and for each of those classes:
    • weight (g): at the midpoint; in the last class, at the midpoint between its lower bound and Linf;
    • leaving: the share of the fish that grow into the next class in a step, dt over the time von Bertalanffy growth takes to cross the class (zero in the last class);
    • ration (g): the food a fish needs in a step, its growth in weight over the step divided by a growth efficiency growth_eff (1 - (w/W∞)^growth_eff_decay);
    • mature: the share mature, logistic in length with slope kappa (one in the last class, zero below eps); spawners is mature × weight;
    • natural: background mortality per step, Nmort dt in the classes from floor(prop × last) on, zero below;
    • catchability: fishing mortality per year at a fishing effort of one, logistic in length (see LEMANS_NORTH_SEA) evaluated at the upper bound of the class, divided by its value at the largest length class of the model, zero below 10⁻⁵. With selectivity_at = :mid it is evaluated at the midpoint instead. LeMaRns 0.1.2 uses the upper bound: LeMansParam passes the midpoints and bounds to calc_Q by position, in an order that gives its argument mid the upper bounds;
    • suitability: a matrix with one row for each predator class of the community (the rows of predators) and one column for each class of this species, the share of the diet of that predator this class would make up if every prey class were equally abundant: exp(-(log10(w_prey / w_predator) - pred_mu)² / (2 pred_sigma)) where the diet matrix allows it, scaled to sum to one over all prey of the predator. Predators in the smallest length class eat nothing;
    and its gear, recruitment parameters a and b, and Linf;
  • predators: the predator classes of the community, (species, class) pairs, species by species and class by class; the pools of lemans_fish have one value for each;
  • other (g), the food outside the model, and eps, added to every mortality rate as in LeMaRns.

pred_sigma divides the squared log-ratio directly, as in LeMaRns, so it is a variance rather than a standard deviation. LeMaRns computes the growth efficiency of the last length class with a fixed 0.5 in place of growth_eff; the two agree at the default.

A size-spectrum fish community (mizer)

MatrixCommunityModels.MIZER_NORTH_SEA — Constant
MIZER_NORTH_SEA

The North Sea model of the R package mizer 3.4.0 (its NS_params, after Blanchard et al. 2014; GPL-3), read from data/mizer, for mizer_parameters. A NamedTuple:

  • species (symbols such as :cod, :n_pout) and names ("Cod", "N.pout"), 12 of them;
  • one vector per species parameter of mizer, in mizer's names and units (g, years): w_min (egg weight), w_mat, w_mat25 (weights at which half and a quarter are mature), w_max, w_repro_max, beta, sigma (the predation kernel), alpha (assimilation efficiency), gamma, q (search volume γ w^q), h, n (maximum intake h w^n), ks, p, k (metabolism k_s w^p + k w), m (the exponent of the reproductive investment), z0 (external mortality per year), erepro (reproductive efficiency), R_max, interaction_resource and k_vb;
  • interaction: the matrix θ, [i, j] the availability of species j to predator i;
  • gear, knife_edge_size, catchability: the fleet that catches each species (one per species), the weight (g) from which it is caught, and its catchability;
  • effort: the fishing effort stored with NS_params, by fleet;
  • resource: the weight grid (min_w, max_w, no_w, min_w_pp) and the resource parameters kappa, lambda, r_pp, n and w_pp_cutoff;
  • initial: the state stored with NS_params, numbers per g by class (fish[:cod], resource).

MatrixCommunityModels.mizer_fish — Method
mizer_fish(p, species)

One species of the mizer model with parameters p (see mizer_parameters), as an OpenCommunity that meets the others through two pools with a value for each predator class of the community (the fish classes of every species, species by species):

  • prey: the prey biomass each predator class meets, Σ φ(w / w_p) w_p θ N(w_p) over the classes of every species and of the resource (mizer's available energy);
  • consumers: the search volume of the predators of each class, γ w^q N(w), which their feeding level turns into the rate at which they remove prey.

The species adds its fish to both and reads both. Its stages are its weight classes, the cells of p.fish[species].stage (weight1, ...), holding numbers of fish, and it has three transitions, whose values are mizer's implicit upwind step (see src/mizer.jl):

  • staying: the fish of a class that survive the step in their class;
  • growing: those that survive and end the step in a larger class;
  • spawning: the eggs, R_dd dt a step, which enter the smallest class and die and grow in the same step as the fish there do, shared among the spawners in proportion to the energy they put into reproduction. Marked as births.

The environment of a step is a NamedTuple with effort, the fishing effort by fleet ((otter = 1.0,), missing fleets not fishing); see mizer_environment.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.mizer_parameters — Function
mizer_parameters(data = MIZER_NORTH_SEA; dt = 0.1)

The quantities of the mizer model derived from data as mizer derives them for NS_params, for a time step of dt years (mizer's default). A NamedTuple with

  • species, names, gears and dt;
  • w, dw: the lower edge and width (g) of the fish weight classes, equally spaced in log weight; w_full, dw_full: those of all classes, the resource's below the fish classes and then the fish classes; offset, the number of resource-only classes;
  • fish: one NamedTuple per species (p.fish.cod) with its ContinuousStage stage (cells weight1, ..., one per fish class; its mesh points are log10 of the geometric middle of each class) and, by class, search_vol (γ w^q), intake_max (h w^n), metab, psi (the share of energy put into reproduction: maturity, zero below 10⁻⁸, times (w / w_repro_max)^(m - n), at most one, and one above w_repro_max), mu_b (external mortality), selectivity, and its gear, catchability, alpha, erepro, R_max, w_min and interaction_resource;
  • kernel: one row per species, the predation kernel φ_i(w_full[1 + m] / w_full[1]) at the m-th class offset, exp(-(log(ratio) - log β)² / (2σ²)), zero at offset zero;
  • interaction; mask, whether each class of w_full is below each species' w_max;
  • resource: its ContinuousStage stage (cells resource1, ...), rate (r_pp w^(n - 1)) and capacity (κ w^-λ below w_pp_cutoff, zero above), by class.

MatrixCommunityModels.mizer_resource — Method
mizer_resource(p; name = :resource)

The background resource of the mizer model with parameters p (plankton, by weight class from the smallest class of p.w_full to the cutoff), as an OpenCommunity. Its stages are the cells of p.resource.stage (resource1, ...), numbers in each class, and it follows mizer's semichemostat: in a class with growth rate r and capacity c (numbers c Δw), eaten at the rate μ read from the pools prey and consumers, a share exp(-(r + μ) dt) of the organisms stays (renewing) and r c Δw (1 - exp(-(r + μ) dt)) / (r + μ) are added (an inflow), the exact solution over a step with the rates held fixed. It adds itself to the pool prey.
