Competitors, predators and pathogens

Flour beetles, a predator and its prey, and chytrid fungus in leopard frogs.

Flour beetles (competition LPA)

MatrixCommunityModels.TRIBOLIUM_DESHARNAIS_2005 — Constant
TRIBOLIUM_DESHARNAIS_2005

Parameters of the competition LPA model of Desharnais et al. (2005, J. Difference Equ. Appl. 11:311-325, Table 1), species 1 Tribolium castaneum and species 2 T. confusum. The coefficients there already include the habitat size, so V = 1. Indexing as in TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003: c_el[i, j] is the paper's c_el,ij, eating of species i's eggs by larvae of species j. The paper varies c_el[2, 1] from 0 to 0.10.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003 — Constant
TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003

Parameters of the competition LPA model of Edmunds et al. (2003, J. Anim. Ecol. 72:703-712), species 1 Tribolium confusum (:confusum) and species 2 T. castaneum (:castaneum), in an 8 g habitat (V = 0.4, in units of 20 g of flour). Coefficient matrices are indexed [victim, eater]: c_el[i, j] is the rate at which larvae of species j eat eggs of species i, c_ea the same for adults, and c_pa[i, j] the rate at which adults of j eat pupae of i. The model divides each coefficient by V.

The paper varies the interspecific egg-eating by larvae, c_el[1, 2] = c_el[2, 1] (its ceL = cEl), from 0.016 to 0.032; the value here, 0.01, is the one it lists before doing so. Vignette 21 explores the paper's range.

MatrixCommunityModels.flour_beetle — Method
flour_beetle(name; b, mu_l, mu_a, eats = [])

One flour beetle species as the LPA net of Dennis et al. (1995): stages L (feeding larvae), P (non-feeding larvae, pupae and callow adults) and A (adults), and a time step of two weeks. Transitions are recruitment (adults to larvae, b per adult, marked as births), larval_survival (1 - mu_l), emergence (pupae to adults) and adult_survival (1 - mu_a).

Recruitment is multiplied by exp(-pool) for the pool <name>_eggs, and emergence by exp(-pool) for the pool <name>_pupae: the shares of eggs and pupae that escape being eaten. The beetle adds to these pools, of its own species and of others, what it eats: eats lists victim => (larvae_eggs = ..., adults_eggs = ..., adults_pupae = ...), per-capita rates of eating the victim's eggs (by this species' larvae and adults) and pupae (by its adults). Include name itself for cannibalism. Rates are used as given (divide by the habitat size first).

MatrixCommunityModels.flour_beetle_competition — Function
flour_beetle_competition(parameters = TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003)

The two-species competition LPA model (Edmunds et al. 2003; Desharnais et al. 2005): two flour_beetles that eat each other's eggs and pupae as well as their own, composed through the four pools <species>_eggs and <species>_pupae. parameters is a set such as TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003 or TRIBOLIUM_DESHARNAIS_2005; change one with merge, for example merge(TRIBOLIUM_EDMUNDS_2003, (c_el = [0.01 0.03; 0.03 0.01],)). The state of each species is [L, P, A].

Predator and prey

MatrixCommunityModels.BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019 — Constant
BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019

The first parameter set of Barraquand & Gimenez (2019, Ecol. Model. 411:108713, Table 5): alpha holds α1 to α8, where newborn predator survival is logistic(α1 + α2 × adult predators), newborn prey survival logistic(α3 + α4 × adult predators), predator fecundity exp(α5 + α6 × juvenile prey) and prey fecundity exp(α7 + α8 × adult prey). adult_survival is the constant adult survival of prey and predator, and n0 the initial juvenile and adult prey and juvenile and adult predators.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019_SET2 — Constant
BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019_SET2

The second parameter set of Barraquand & Gimenez (2019, Appendices A and B): as BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019 but α5 = 0.5, α6 = 0.01 and α7 = 1.5, a stronger coupling of predator and prey.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.predator_prey — Function
predator_prey(parameters = BARRAQUAND_GIMENEZ_2019)

The two-species, two-stage predator-prey model of Barraquand & Gimenez (2019): a pre-breeding census, so that an adult's newborns are counted as juveniles a year later only if they survive, and juveniles become adults in one year. Each species is a net with stages juvenile and adult and transitions reproduction (adults to juveniles, half the fecundity times newborn survival, marked as births), maturation and adult_survival (both at the constant adult survival). The prey occupies the pool juvenile_prey and reads adult_predators; the predator occupies adult_predators and reads juvenile_prey.

Returns (community, n0). Built with births marked and survival probabilities, the community can be projected with demographic stochasticity (project(..., rng = ...)), which gives the binomial survival and Poisson recruitment of the paper's stochastic model.

Chytrid fungus in leopard frogs

MatrixCommunityModels.CHYTRID_SITES — Constant
CHYTRID_SITES

The four locations of Wilber et al. (2022), for leopard_frog: PA (Pennsylvania) and VT (Vermont), with the northern leopard frog Rana pipiens, and LA (Louisiana) and TN (Tennessee), with the southern leopard frog R. sphenocephala. Each is a NamedTuple with

  • location, host, model (the fitted model whose medians are used: :a_temp, Bd growth depending on temperature, or :both_model, Bd growth and transmission depending on temperature) and temperature, the yearly minimum and maximum water temperature (°C) of the sinusoid;
  • s0, the weekly survival of an uninfected post-metamorphic frog, and sI, the survival of an infected frog relative to it;
  • a, a_temp, b, sigma: the log load next week is normal with mean a + a_temp (T - 15) + b x for log load x now and standard deviation sigma; a new infection starts with the load a + a_temp (T - 15) and the same deviation;
  • mu_l, sigma_l: an infected frog of log load x loses its infection within the week with chance 1 - Φ((x - mu_l) / sigma_l);
  • beta, beta_temp: a susceptible frog in the water is infected within the week with chance 1 - exp(-beta exp(beta_temp (T - 15)) Z / area) for Z zoospores;
  • shedding: zoospores shed in a week by a frog of log load x in the water, shedding × e^x;
  • fecundity: tadpoles per post-metamorphic frog at the breeding pulse;
  • K: recruitment of metamorphs is multiplied by exp(-e^K N / area) for N post-metamorphic frogs;
  • larval_period (days), breeding (first and last day of the breeding season), aquatic (whether post-metamorphic frogs live in the water all year), hibernation, hibernation_aquatic and hibernation_period (first and last day, which may wrap round the year's end).

a, a_temp, sigma, mu_l and beta (and beta_temp for LA) are medians of the posterior fitted by the authors; the rest are fixed values of their parameter files. Sources of the values are in the comments of src/chytrid.jl.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.CHYTRID_ZOOSPORE_SURVIVAL — Constant
CHYTRID_ZOOSPORE_SURVIVAL

The weekly survival of Bd zoospores in water at temperature T (°C) used by chytrid_zoospores: maxsurv / (1 + exp((T - ld50) / s)), a NamedTuple with ld50, s and maxsurv. The authors' code fits this curve by least squares (scipy's fmin, from (20, 1)) to the weekly survival exp(-7 × 24 × rate) at the four temperatures 4, 14.5, 23 and 28 °C, with the death rates per hour 0.0027, 0.0035, 0.0160 and 0.0410 that it attributes to Woodhams et al. (2008); maxsurv is the largest of the four. The values are those of that fit, read back from the authors' function.

chytrid_community(site; area = 500, cells = 300)

The leopard frog of site (leopard_frog) and the Bd zoospores in its water (chytrid_zoospores), composed through the pools zoospores and shedding: the full IPM of Wilber et al. (2022) for one host species. The pool frogs stays exposed, so that another host can be added.

chytrid_environment(site, steps; start = 0)

MatrixCommunityModels.chytrid_temperature — Method
chytrid_temperature(site, day)

The water temperature (°C) on day (counted from the start of a run, which is the coldest day) at site (a key of CHYTRID_SITES or a site NamedTuple): the authors' sinusoid, min + (max - min) (1 - cos(2π day / 364)) / 2.

MatrixCommunityModels.chytrid_zoospores — Method
chytrid_zoospores(; survival = CHYTRID_ZOOSPORE_SURVIVAL, name = :zoospores)

The zoospores of Bd in the water, as an OpenCommunity of one stage, zoospores, for chytrid_community. Each week a share survival.maxsurv / (1 + exp((T - survival.ld50) / survival.s)) of them survives, at the water temperature T of the step's environment (see CHYTRID_ZOOSPORE_SURVIVAL), and the zoospores shed by the hosts, the pool shedding, are added (an inflow). Its zoospores are the pool zoospores, which hosts read to know how many they meet.

MatrixCommunityModels.leopard_frog — Method
leopard_frog(site; area = 500, cells = 300, name = :frog, crowding = :frogs)

The leopard frog at site (a key of CHYTRID_SITES such as :PA, or a site NamedTuple) in the full host-parasite integral projection model of Wilber et al. (2022), as an OpenCommunity of one species. Numbers are totals in an arena of area m³ and the time step is 7 days.

The stages are tadpole, susceptible (uninfected post-metamorphic frogs) and the cells of chytrid_load_stage (load1, ...), infected frogs by the logarithm of their Bd load. The transitions are

  • developing and metamorphosing: a tadpole survives the week with chance 0.2^(7 / larval_period) (a fifth survive the larval period) and metamorphoses with chance 7 / larval_period, the metamorphs damped by exp(-e^K N / area), where N is the pool named by crowding (frogs);
  • escaping and infecting: a susceptible frog survives (s0) and is infected with the chance given in CHYTRID_SITES, which reads the pool zoospores, while in the water; an infection starts at a load drawn from the distribution of a new infection;
  • growing: an infected frog survives (sI s0), keeps its infection and its load changes;
  • clearing: an infected frog survives and loses its infection, becoming susceptible;
  • breeding: at the breeding pulse every post-metamorphic frog, infected or not, produces fecundity tadpoles. Marked as births.

As in the authors' code the load kernels are integrated over each cell of the next load (a difference of normal distribution functions), not evaluated at its middle. The environment of a step is (day, temperature) (see chytrid_environment).

The species adds its post-metamorphic frogs to the pool named by crowding, and the zoospores its infected frogs shed in the week, shedding × Σ e^x, to the pool shedding while they are in the water. It reads the pool zoospores, which chytrid_zoospores provides; chytrid_community composes the two. In the authors' model the recruitment of a species is damped by its own frogs only, so two hosts composed into one community should be given pools of their own (crowding = :pipiens_frogs, say); with the same pool, each host's recruitment would be damped by both.

Canvasbacks and redheads

MatrixCommunityModels.WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025 — Constant
WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025

The canvasback and redhead model of Gibson, Arnold, Buderman & Koons (2025, Ecol. Monogr. 95:e1642) at the posterior means of the authors' 8000 draws (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.hqbzkh1n9, CC0; see data/waterfowl):

  • rates.<species>: for 1961 to 2020, the yearly per-female fecundity (ducklings of both sexes per older female) and, for females (_f) and males (_m), natural winter survival of hatch-years (sw_hy) and adults (sw_ahy), direct harvest probability (kappa_hy, kappa_ahy) and natural summer survival (ss).
  • omega: breeding success of yearling relative to older females, and indirect_harvest, the ratio exp(β_indirect) of the indirect to the direct harvest hazard of adults. The draws do not store these; each draw's value is recovered from its abundances and the posterior mean taken.
  • bp_ltre: the mean of the bp column used by the authors' LTRE script (it is not aligned with the draws).
  • n0, abundance: posterior means of the breeding numbers (millions) of yearling and older females and males, 1961 to 2021, with 5% and 95% quantiles of the total.
  • covariates: the breeding female numbers and the canvasback-to-redhead female ratio that the fitted model took as data, with the means and standard deviations it standardised them by (density_mean, density_sd, ratio_mean, ratio_sd), and the breeding survey estimates (bpop), 1961 to 2021 (no survey in 2020 and 2021).
  • beta_density, beta_ratio: the effects of conspecific female density and of female canvasbacks per female redhead on log fecundity, as printed in Appendix S1, Table S6 (used only by the feedback extension).
source
MatrixCommunityModels.waterfowl_community — Function
waterfowl_community(parameters = WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025; feedback = false)

Canvasbacks and redheads (waterfowl_duck) at the parameters of WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025. Project through waterfowl_environment. With feedback = false (the published model) the species do not meet in the projection: their yearly rates, which the paper fitted with the densities and the canvasback-to-redhead ratio as data, are the environment. With feedback = true, an extension that is not the published model, fecundity responds to the projected numbers of breeding females of both species through the pools canvasback_females and redhead_females, with the slopes of Appendix S1, Table S6 (see waterfowl_duck). Change the slopes with merge, e.g. merge(WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025, (beta_ratio = (canvasback = 0.0, redhead = 0.0),)) for conspecific density dependence alone.

Returns (community, n0), n0 the posterior-mean breeding numbers of 1961 (millions).

source
MatrixCommunityModels.waterfowl_duck — Method
waterfowl_duck(name; omega, indirect_harvest, feedback = nothing)

One duck species of the model of Gibson et al. (2025), with stages sy_female, asy_female, sy_male and asy_male (breeding yearlings and older birds, counted in May) and transitions female_recruitment and male_recruitment (females to yearlings of each sex: half the ducklings, omega per yearling female and one per older female, marked as births) and female_survival and male_survival (yearlings and older birds to older birds). The rates of each year come from the environment of the step, the field name of waterfowl_environment: recruitment is fecundity times natural winter survival times 1 - harvest of hatch-years times the square root of next year's summer survival; survival is the square root of this summer's survival times natural winter survival times (1 - harvest)^indirect_harvest times the square root of next year's summer survival.

With feedback = (beta_density, beta_ratio, density_sd, ratio_sd), an extension that is not the published model, fecundity is multiplied by exp(β_N (N - N_ref) / sd_N + β_C/R (C/R - C_ref/R_ref) / sd_C/R): N is the number of breeding females of this species, read from the pool <name>_females, C/R the ratio of the pools canvasback_females and redhead_females, and N_ref, C_ref, R_ref the posterior-mean numbers of the fit that year (reference in the environment). The slopes are those of the fitted fecundity model, per standard deviation of the covariate, so the published fecundity is recovered when the projection follows the fit. The species then occupies <name>_females and reads the other species' pool.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.waterfowl_environment — Function
waterfowl_environment(parameters = WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025; years = 1961:2019)

The environment of each yearly step from May of year to May of year + 1: a named tuple with year and, for each species, its rates of that year (see WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025) with ss_f_next and ss_m_next, the summer survival of the next year, and reference, the posterior-mean numbers of breeding females of both species that year (millions), from which the feedback extension measures its departures. The rates end in 2020, so the last step starts in 2019.

source
MatrixCommunityModels.waterfowl_ltre — Function
waterfowl_ltre(parameters = WATERFOWL_GIBSON_2025)

The transient life table response experiment of Gibson et al. (2025, Equation 35), transcribed from the authors' LTRECode.R and evaluated on the posterior means instead of each draw. For each species, the 18 yearly series of the authors' script (fecundity of yearling and older females F1, F2; female and male hatch-year and adult winter (`w), summer (s) and harvest (h) survival; the proportions of the breeding population in each class,nf1,nf2,nm1,nm2`) are formed as the script forms them, sensitivities of the realised growth rate are evaluated at their temporal means with the script's formulas, and the contribution of each series to the variance of the growth rate is the sum over the others of covariance times both sensitivities.

Returns a named tuple per species with names (the 18 series), contribution, sensitivity, total and lambda, the geometric mean growth rate of the whole breeding population. On the posterior means the contributions differ from the paper's, which average this calculation over the draws (vignette 28).
