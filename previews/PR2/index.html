MatrixCommunityModels.jl

Matrix community models (MCMs; Lytle & Tonkin 2023) are sets of single-species matrix population models linked by aggregate density dependence. This package builds them by composition: each species is a projection net of named transitions, with responses to shared pools (space, biomass, aggregate density), and a community is the composite of species along an undirected wiring diagram whose junctions are the pools. Communities are projected by MatrixProjectionModels.jl, or, when a species has a continuous stage (an integral projection model), by IntegralProjectionModels.jl.

It is built on the ecorecipes packages MatrixProjectionModels.jl, IntegralProjectionModels.jl, ProjectionModels.jl and CategoricalPopulationDynamics.jl, and on Catlab.jl.

Tutorials

  • Matrix community models by composition: species, pools, composition, projection, and freezing a community into ordinary matrices.
  • Verde River fish: the seven-species model of Rogosch et al. (2019), identical to the original R output.
  • Riparian plant guilds: the five-guild model of Lytle et al. (2017), the original and a corrected variant.
  • Two orchids: the community projection of the adapt3 R package, reproduced.
  • Cross-species sensitivity: interactions revealed by perturbing vital rates, and the resulting network.
  • Composing IPMs: an integral projection model and a matrix model sharing a pool, kernels that respond to pools, and a seed bank.
  • Metacommunity: patches as nested communities, with dispersal as a pool.
  • Equilibria, invasion and chance: analytic sensitivities, invasion rates in fluctuating environments, what would let an excluded species persist (the sensitivity of its invasion rate), demographic stochasticity.
  • Seasons and phenology: within-year steps, a lottery for space, coexistence by timing, and drift.
  • Bees: a honey bee colony, Varroa and deformed wing virus, a bumblebee population and sharing flowers: a community built step by step.
  • Thistles and a weevil: a published integral projection model of a native thistle, a published matrix model of an invasive thistle, and the weevil they share.
  • A forest on a large mesh: three tree species as integral projection models on 700 cells each, coupled through stand basal area and checked against the R package matreex.
  • Sea fans and aspergillosis: a published integral projection model stratified by infection status, with healthy, infected and resistant colonies as strata of one life cycle.
  • Four perennials of the sagebrush steppe: the integral projection models of Adler, Ellner & Levine (2010), coupled by crowding through each species' cover and checked against the R package IPMdoit; coexistence, invasion when rare and the removal of niche differences.
  • North Sea fish by length: the length-based multispecies model LeMans (Hall et al. 2006) for 21 species, with predation by predator and prey size through pools that hold a value for each predator length class, checked against the R package LeMaRns; the unfished community and fishing by four fleets.
  • Corals of Mo'orea: the open-population integral projection models of Kayal et al. (2018) for three coral genera recolonising four reefs, with recruits arriving as inflows in numbers set by crowding, checked against the authors' kernel functions and the published simulations of reef recovery.
  • Five grasslands: the multispecies integral projection models of Chu & Adler (2015) for five sites, built with the sagebrush machinery and checked against the authors' Kansas runs; coexistence, invasion in drawn years and the synchrony of growth rates (Tredennick et al. 2017).
  • Two flour beetles: the competition LPA model of Edmunds et al. (2003) and Desharnais et al. (2005), two larva-pupa-adult models coupled by eating each other's eggs and pupae; the published equilibria, bifurcations, coexistence two-cycle and stochastic outcomes.
  • Designed flow regimes: riparian plants, fish and three aquatic invertebrates under flows designed for each and under the natural regime (Tonkin et al. 2021), checked against the authors' R code.
  • Predator and prey: the two-species, two-stage matrix model of Barraquand & Gimenez (2019), checked against the authors' difference equations; fixed points, oscillations and demographic noise.
  • Chytrid fungus in leopard frogs: the full host-parasite integral projection model of Wilber et al. (2022), with Bd load as the continuous trait, a zoospore component joined by pools, and seasonal temperature and breeding at four locations, checked against the authors' Python code; persistence with demographic stochasticity.
  • A size-spectrum fish community: the North Sea model of the R package mizer, its implicit upwind step written exactly as a projection matrix, with predation through pools that hold a value for each of 1200 predator classes, checked against mizer's runs and steady states; fishing, the size spectrum and a comparison with LeMans.

Each tutorial is a Quarto vignette, also available as HTML and PDF in the repository.

Quick start

using MatrixCommunityModels

K = 1000.0
crowding(ctx) = max(0.0, 1 - ctx.pool.space / K)

# a life cycle is a net of named transitions
grass_life = ValuedProjectionNet([:seedling, :adult],
    :establishing => [(:seedling => :adult) => 0.3],
    :persisting   => [(:adult => :adult) => 0.5],
    :seeding      => [(:adult => :seedling) => 6.0])
shrub_life = ValuedProjectionNet([:seedling, :adult],
    :establishing => [(:seedling => :adult) => 0.1],
    :persisting   => [(:adult => :adult) => 0.95],
    :seeding      => [(:adult => :seedling) => 3.0])

# a response says how a transition depends on a shared pool
grass = species(:grass, respond(grass_life, :seeding => crowding); occupies = [:space => [:seedling => 0.1, :adult => 1.0]])
shrub = species(:shrub, respond(shrub_life, :seeding => crowding); occupies = [:space => [:seedling => 0.1, :adult => 4.0]])

com = community(grass, shrub)
sol = project(com, [:grass => [50.0, 10.0], :shrub => [5.0, 1.0]], fill(nothing, 200))
sol[:shrub]            # stage-by-time matrix
